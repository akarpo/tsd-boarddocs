*Troy School Board: Workshop Meeting, November 10, 2026*

Meeting Recording - https://youtu.be/gQwqU5icsIo

**This three hour, 14 minute workshop opened with the 2025-26 audit, which has the General Fund ending the year just above the Board's 15 percent goal, and spent its longest stretch on special education: more students moving in with IEPs, programs near capacity, and high schools still adjusting to the end of supplanted classes. The rest of the night covered a proposed Plante Moran study of how every building is used, early steel for the Athens addition, the first leased fleet trucks, the Digital Delusion plan, returning tests, modified grades, a pause on schools of choice, and two items from the Superintendent.**

**The audit: the General Fund lands above 15 percent**

Dan Trudel presented with Kyle Anderson, the district's new Finance Director; Plante & Moran gives the formal presentation at next week's regular meeting. The results: a clean (unmodified) opinion and no findings in the federal single audit, bond and sinking fund compliance, or the Budgeting Act.

· General Fund: revenue of $199,809,780, spending of $202,195,371, and a fund balance of $31,328,204, or 15.49 percent of expenditures. The original and amended budgets had it dipping below 14 percent. Trudel credited positions left unfilled mid-year and careful building budgets: "So kudos to everybody around this organization."
· The year's deficit came in at $2,385,591 against $5,488,478 in the final budget. Dr. Machesky said that eases this year's reductions: "Couple million off on a $100 to $200 million budget is pretty darn good."
· Over $1 million in support has been restored this year, including elementary secretaries, teacher consultants and eight student support aides for students without IEPs (almost 160 aide positions were part of the $12 million in cuts). The first budget amendment is targeted for January.
· Direct instruction is 64.72 percent of General Fund spending, against an Oakland County average of 58.27 percent. The fall count is running slightly above the 11,869 students budgeted, and the foundation allowance is $11,086 per student.
· The first bond series has earned close to $30 million in interest, of which $8 to $9 million goes to the IRS in about two years under the arbitrage rules; the second series is invested to stay under that limit.

**A study of how every building is used**

Keith McDonald of Plante Moran Realpoint, a former deputy and interim superintendent at Bloomfield Hills, proposed a building utilization study: school-by-school enrollment projections, a room-by-room classification of every building, capacity and operating costs per building, in about two months. He said the district lost almost 1,100 students from 2017-18 to last year, and the firm's last projection shows a loss of about 2,000 between 2019 and 2030. An underused building costs about $10 a square foot a year.

Trudel tied it to the schools of choice decision, the new middle schools and the special education restructuring: "This is a multi-year planning strategy." Vital Anne questioned the timing:

*"Shouldn't we be doing this study and review before we build a brand new building so that we know what we should be building?" (Vital Anne)*

Trudel said the 2022 facilities review was very high level on enrollment, and that the decline is now reaching the elementary schools. Machesky: "It's not just about potentially closing buildings." Repurposing space for special education, adult transition or early childhood are options. Stephanie Zendler asked for a staff communication plan "so we don't create, like, chaos." Audra Melton said an MASB session showed a district using the same kind of study to plan attendance areas, grade configurations and busing. The proposal goes on the next regular meeting agenda; no cost was discussed.

**Athens addition: structural steel ordered early**

Structural steel goes to Commercial Contracting Corporation, the low of six bids, at $7,951,352 plus an 8 percent contingency, $8,587,460.16 in all, ahead of the other trades because of lead times ("We'll blame it on the data centers"). It is the first large purchase from the 2026 Capital Projects Fund. Trudel said the Athens bids overall are coming in $8 to $10 million under a budget of nearly $100 million; the rest come to the December 1 workshop.

**Leasing the first fleet trucks**

The district will lease about 18 vehicles from Enterprise over four years instead of buying replacements, which Trudel called "about a break-even scenario." The first four are two Ford F-350s and two F-550s, quoted at $1,212.40 a month for the F-350 with a plow and $1,783.95 a month for the F-550 dump truck with a plow and spreader, over 60 months from the General Fund.

**Special education: more move-ins, fuller programs**

Sarah Smotherman, Director of Special Education, presented with supervisors Jamie Evans, Erica Conklin and Hillary Pulliam.

· More than 1,400 students have IEPs, 11.7 percent of enrollment. For the first time, autism eligibility exceeds specific learning disability.
· Under the county's revised plan the district now has five Level 2 classrooms (it had none) and eight Level 3 programs (it had three), moving students into less restrictive settings.
· 96 students have transferred in with an IEP this year, against 70 at this point last year, and 27 needed a categorical program. Of 100 schools of choice students with IEPs, 26 are in categorical programs.
· Many Level 3 and Level 4 programs are full or close to it. Describing a resident move-in likely to need a Level 4 seat, Smotherman said her response was: "where do we have space?" Machesky said a new program means a teacher, two paras and possibly aides: "Significant cost to start a program."
· The district now serves preschoolers inside the 17 community programs that run a GSRP, with its own staff and no added funding. "It's an unfunded mandate," Anne said. Zendler: "Funded or not, Child Find is a legal obligation."
· Tier 3 behavior cases have grown from 11 in fall 2023 to 35 this fall; elementary schools now have assigned behavior analysts and whole-class coaching.

Christine DiPilato said the changes are landing on top of the budget cuts, with reduced specialist support and high caseloads, and that the current structure is "not sustainable for anyone who's in the organization right now." Zendler said she was asking as an advocate: "we cut the deepest in our budget cuts in special ed. Like 35% of our cuts came from special ed, our most vulnerable population."

**Digital Delusion: a district philosophy by spring, rollout in fall 2027**

DiPilato laid out the plan: an audit of technology and its cost by winter break, a district philosophy on technology to the Board around January, a survey of students, families and staff in February and March, adoption in spring 2027 and rollout in fall 2027. Emina Alic raised student data and AI in the classroom. On a closed AI system used in grades 3 to 5:

*"the danger isn't whether the system is closed or unclosed. It's the fact that the system is replacing the thought process that the 3rd grader needs to have" (Emina Alic)*

On the online-only athletic passes: "So GoFan knows when a particular child attended a particular game." Anne asked for a process to approve new technology and re-evaluate it every year or two.

**Getting tests back**

DiPilato described error analysis and larger question banks, with test security as the teachers' concern. Alic said test security "sounds like Silicon Valley tech language to me" and noted that Bloomfield Hills returns an assessment when a parent asks. DiPilato called it "work that we need to be able to do with our teachers, not to our teachers." Alic asked that next spring's survey not be a single in-school day and that it include parents; Zendler added teachers.

**Modified grades are back**

A new handbook brings back modified curriculum: the IEP team, with the family, changes the standard itself, and the grade carries an asterisk on the report card and transcript. DiPilato expects it to apply to 1 to 2 percent of students, mostly in high school, now that the state has ended supplanted classes. At Troy High, students with IEPs placed in Algebra 1 were struggling, so a co-taught foundational math course was created this fall; Athens had used intensified algebra. On the difference: "I will take the blame for what that looks like."

**Schools of choice: pause for 2027-28**

Machesky recommended no new schools of choice seats for 2027-28, pending the building study, with a survey of current families about siblings. Trudel said a change in state enrollment stabilization funding brings about $1 million the district did not budget. No trustee objected.

**From the Superintendent**

· The Troy High JV baseball field was not built to its design and will be regraded. A retaining wall of about 60 feet, not in the original design, may cost the district under $200,000, with the contractor's share to be sorted out. It comes to the Board at the December 1 workshop.
· Machesky addressed the deputy superintendent item pulled from the last meeting's consent agenda, saying misinformation had been shared publicly. He said the title reflects added responsibilities, in this case the bond, and that the recommendation was meant to be cost neutral; the conversation will continue later.

**Also at this meeting**

· No public comment.
· MASB conference reports from Melton, Zendler and Anne.
· Alic asked that the Board meet with the area's new state representative, state senator and congressman, with a one-page list of funding issues.
· Anne thanked Walt Cook for running and welcomed Mary Gunn, elected to the Board on November 3. Anne, re-elected: "You're stuck with me for another 6 years."
· The recording ends at about 9:12 p.m.

**One important caveat**

*This was a workshop, so nothing was voted on: the facilities study, the steel award and the leases come back to a regular meeting, and the rest of the Athens bids and the baseball field to the December 1 workshop. The audit figures are Plante & Moran's and the district's, the lease amounts are Enterprise's quotes, and the special education counts are the department's as of the meeting. The recording is the Teams recording of the room camera, not a cable broadcast.*
