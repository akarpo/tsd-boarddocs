B.  CONSIDERATION:   Schools Closed to Open Enrollment for  
                                        2020-2021 
Administration has reviewed the enrollment for elementary, middle, and high 
school attendance areas.  Due to the elementary and middle school  buildings’ 
total student enrollment and physical space constraints, we recommend that 
Barnard, B emis, Schroeder and Boulan Park  Schools be closed to open 
enrollment.  Our goal  for Athens and Troy High  has been to maximize each 
building’s enrollment which has resulted in  both schools being closed to open 
enrollment for the past several years. Based on current enrollment projections 
for the 2020-2021 school year, it is recommended that Athens and Troy High be 
closed to open enrollment with the exception of not more than (25) twenty-five 
incoming ninth grade students who reside in the Troy High School attendance 
area but wish to attend Athens High School.  Applications for the (25) twenty-
five spots at Athens High School will be accepted during a two-week window in 
March. 
RECOMMENDED ACTION: Adopt the following resolution. 
SUGGESTED RESOLUTION 
 Moved by: __________________________ 
 Supported by: __________________________ 
Be it therefore resolved that the Troy Board of Education adopts a resolution to close 
Barnard, Bemis, Schroeder and Boulan Park Schools as well as Athens and Troy High 
Schools to open enrollment for the 2020-2021 school year, with the exception of not 
more than (25) twenty-five incoming ninth grade students who reside in the Troy High 
School attendance area but wish to attend Athens High School.  Students with siblings 
attending Athens and Troy High and currently enrolled in grades 9 – 11 will be allowed 
to open enroll to remain for the 2020-2021 school year.

RE:  Fuel platforms available for school buses 
Rick, 
At your request I will provide a summary of fuel options for school buses today, 
and offer a business case to continue with clean diesel at Troy School District. We 
currently offer clean diesel, propane, CNG, and now a Battery electric EV model. 
We at Hoekstra Transportation feel strongly that clean diesel in the bridge to 
battery electric school buses, but the EV school bus must scale to lower the 
acquisition costs. We are involved in a pilot project to deliver the first EV school 
buses, the Thomas Built Jouley EV, to Ann Arbor and Roseville Schools. The 
project was directed by DTE, partnering with Thomas Built Buses, Proterra (our 
battery, traction drive and charging station supplier), along with our dealership. 
Applications were submitted to EGLE and each school was awarded grant funds 
through the Volkswagon Mitigation fund. This grant covered 70% of the cost of 
the bus, infrastructure and charging stations. The total cost came to 
approximately $360,000 per bus. 
As you can see, without a grant, the EV models are just not ready in price, and I 
might add, yet to be proven reliability and cost of operation – this is why we have 
pilot projects. We feel school bus operations are a great fit for EV because of the 
limited number of miles and predictable returns to the terminal for re-charging. If 
you think about your average mileage of 15,000 annually, at today’s fuel cost of 
around $2.20 per gallon of diesel and 7.5 mpg (conservatively), the total fuel  
costs over a 12 year life cycle would be $52,800. The EV will operate for less, not 
sure exactly how much in electricity, and will have a lower cost of operation – it 
just needs to scale, the cost of batteries will continue to go down, and we need 
proof of concept in terms of operational costs. Over the next decade, EV school 
buses will be a factor.
