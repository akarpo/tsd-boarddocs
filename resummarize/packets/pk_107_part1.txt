2.       RECOGNITION 
A. CAPE (Citizens and Parents for Education) Steering Committee 
     for TSD Building & Site Sinking Fund 
As you no doubt have heard, voters overwhelmingly came out in favor 
of the Troy School District one-mill Building & Site Sinking Fund  in the 
November 7 th Election. The proposal passed with about 68 % of the 
votes, which is a testament to the support of our parents and the Troy 
Community. The results exemplified their faith in our schools and the 
value they see in the exemplary education we provide for the students 
of this District. We are very grateful for their c onfidence in the fiscal 
responsibility of our Board of Education and their trust in our 
stewardship of their investment. 
Passage of the Sinking Fund could not have come without the tireless 
work of many people  — parents, community volunteers, staff and 
administrators. Tonight, we would like to personally thank the driving 
force behind the TSD Building & Site Sinking Fund —the Steering 
Committee of CAPE ( Citizens and Parents for Education ), who 
volunteered countless hours of their time to ensure that this initiative 
passed. Their dedication and hard work—both behind the scenes and in 
public forums—was integral to the success of the Sinking Fund Proposal. 
We would like to especially acknowledge CAPE chair Tracy Heikka, who 
coordinated the group’s efforts and  worked tirelessly to ensure this 
important measure passed.  
Mrs. Heikka would say it took a team, so t onight, we are pleased to 
recognize the CAPE Steering Committee for their exemplary service  to 
the Troy School District.  
Please come up as we read your name and then we would like Mrs. 
Heikka to come up at the end and say a few words, if she wishes.

2016-2017 Audit Report David  Youngstrom,  Audit  Partner  from  Yeo  & Yeo, 
provided a presentation to the Board of Education on 
the audit of the 2016-2017 fiscal year. A draft copy of 
the District’s Audit Report was presented in detail to the 
Board at the special workshop earlier this evening. Mr. 
Youngstrom reviewed governmental revenue and 
expenditures, general fund revenue and expenditures, 
instructional expenditures comparison to Oakland 
County Schools, the general and historical fund 
balance,  and  the  history  of  the  fund  balance  as a

percentage of expenditures. He reviewed internal 
control matters, material weaknesses or deficiencies, 
compliance findings, and future challenges. Some of 
the challenges he mentione d are the cost of fringe 
benefits, state economic/political conditions, retirement 
rate increases, the uncer tainty of state revenue 
sources, special education costs, the unfunded pension 
liability and the new accounting standards. 
Mr. Hauff asked how much time is put into conducting 
the audit. Mr. Youngstrom shared that 3–4 auditors are 
onsite for three weeks and spend approximately 3-400 
hours working on the audit. Mr. Youngstrom thanked 
Mrs. Trotter, Mr. West, and their staff for their effort 
and for being prepared. Mr. Schmidt wanted to clarify 
that the fund balance is more of a savings account and 
used as needed before funds are received from the 
government.
