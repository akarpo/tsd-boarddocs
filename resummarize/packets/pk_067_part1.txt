1. ELECTION OF ACTING CHAIRPERSON 
The first order of business is to e lect an acting chairperson who will 
conduct the meeting until such time as a president has been elected.  Past 
practice has been that the previous year’s president conducts this part 
of the meeting. 
2. APPOINTMENT OF TEMPORARY SECRETARY 
 The second order of business is for the acting chairperson  to 
 appoint Judy Holmes to act as temporary secretary until such time as the 
 secretary to the Board has been elected. 
3. ELECTION OF PRESIDENT 
The acting chairperson declares nominations in order for the office o f 
president of the board of education (please note, nominations need not be 
seconded; and a motion and support are made to close the nomination.)  
Please understand that four votes are required for election. 
4. ELECTION OF VICE PRESIDENT 
The newly elected  president declares nominations in order for the 
office of vice president, following the same procedures as outlined for the 
election of president. 
5. ELECTION OF A SECRETARY 
The president declares nominations in order for the office of secretary, 
following the same procedures as outlined for the election of president. 
6. APPOINTMENT OF TREASURER 
According to Board Policy, the treasurer is appointed annually by the 
Board as a nonvoting officer.  The assistant superintendent of business 
services has served  as treasurer of the Board.  It is my recommendation 
that this procedure continue.

B.   TSD Mission Moment—Athens High School Urban Farming Initiative  
The Pingree 4-H program is an urban farming initiative that gives students in the 
Troy School District the opportunity to raise animals for competition in the Oakland 
County Fair. The program, under the leadership of teacher Christina Harlan, brings 
together special education and general education students from Athens High School 
and Baker and Larson Middle Schools. The farm is located in the City of Detroit, and 
every Friday through the school year, students, parent volunteers and Miss Harlan 
gather to feed, clean and care for their assigned animal. 
The students then show the mini -cows, horses, goats, chickens and a variety of 
other animals a t the Oakland County Fair in the summer. Last year, they brought 
home many awards, but more than that, learned valuable lessons about farming, 
responsibility, independence and the teamwork it takes to run a farm effectively.  
We would like to invite teacher Christina Harlan and her 4-H students up to receive 
their certificates.

Connectors 
Tim Fulcher, Smith principal, invited Ray Torres and Jen 
Hartenburg,  the  advisors,  to  introduce  the  Smith 
Connectors.   The  Smith  Connectors  is  a  mentoring 
program that consists of 8th grade students who serve as 
leaders  at  Smith  Middle  School  as  well  as  in  the 
community.  The  Smith  Connectors  are  held  to  a  high 
expectation  of  leadership,  respect,  and  responsibility.  
Their actions and choices both in and out of school are 
expected  to  uphold  the  standards  of  Smith.   The 
Connectors  are  involved  in  many  leadership  activities 
throughout  the  school  year  such  as  assisting  at 
Adventure  Island,  helping  6th  graders  adjust  to  middle 
school,  teambuilding  and  anti‐bullying  activities.   They 
help to create a positive and safe culture at Smith.  They 
also  provide  tours  to  5th  graders  who  will  be  entering 
middle  school  the  following  year  and  participate  in 
various  community  service  projects.   These  8th  grade 
students  are  role  models  for  the  other  students  at
