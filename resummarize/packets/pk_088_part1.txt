A.   TSD Mission Moment—Ed Morykwas 
Tonight we honor someone who has become an integral part of the Troy 
School District, going above and beyond the call of duty to help showcase our 
students and staff and tell our stories. Ed Morykwas is retiring as the 
photographer/photojournalist/webmaster of School Life Troy, our very popular 
monthly newspaper. Ed helped to launch School Life two years ago and in that 
time, became a regular fixture in every school, at every event and in every 
celebration. With his trademark enthusiasm, talent and passion for the work 
that goes on in our classrooms, Ed has helped shine a spotlight on the 
achievements and efforts of our students and staff. His photos tell our stories, 
but his personality and never -ending dedication builds confidence for our 
students and shapes the way we see ourselves. 
Ed, we thank you for your exemplary service and commitment to the Troy 
School District and we wish you well in your retirement. Next year, there will 
be a new photographer, we know, but no one will be able to fill your unique 
role here in our hearts. 
At this time, we would like to invite Ed to say a few words.

Call for Participants!  
Application Due: Sunday, September 30, 2015 
The Center for Peace and Conflict Studies ((http://clas.wayne.edu/cpcs/) at Wayne State 
University, as part of its celebratory 50th Anniversary year, invites high-school teachers and 
educators to join our educational program entitled “Discovering Japan in the 21s Century: 
Reconciliation with the Past.” The program, sponsored by the US-Japan Foundation (http://us-
jf.org/), is presented free of charge to the participants, and will include cultural and informational 
training sessions after school hours and will culminate in a 10-day study tour of Japan in May of 
2016. 
We will select ten participants from the applicants who are interested in learning about present 
day Japan and its efforts to reconcile with the past, with its neighborhoods, and with the United 
States at the 70th anniversary of the end of Asia-Pacific War. The aim is to stimulate educational 
programming for Detroit area students and classrooms; teachers will be asked to devise and 
implement appropriate lesson plans from their experience. 
Requirements: 
1. Be interested in Japan’s historical issues and reconciliation efforts 
2. Be eager to learn about Japanese culture and contemporary Japanese issues 
3. Attend pre-tour sessions including public lectures and other curriculum between October 
2015 and April 2016 
4. Be willing to travel to Japan for the 10 days in May 2016 with permission of your school  
5. Be willing to write a report and teaching manual on Japan’s historical efforts on 
reconciliation with the past 
6. Be willing to collaborate with others  
Duties: 
1. Attend an inaugural event on September 24, 2015 
(https://www.facebook.com/US.JP.CPCS): Open to the Public.  
2. Fully participate in the 7-month bi-weekly training sessions (including public lectures) 
during after-school or weekend hours between October 2015 and April 2016  
3. Prepare a research topic to explore in Japan 
4. Travel to Japan for 10 days in Mid-May 2016 
5. Write a report and teaching manual based on experience and findings, and participate in 
ongoing social media and local district presentation and discussion 
6. Give a presentation on research finding at a Wayne State min-conference in June 2016 
Benefits: 
1. Training and travel support (including airfare, transportation, accommodation, travel 
insurance, and meals in Japan)  
2. Chance to meet Japanese political representatives, diplomats, and scholars  
3. Interaction with peer Japanese high-school teachers and students

D.  Spirit of Diversity Awards  
The Board of Education is proud to recognize eighteen individuals for our 
Spirit of Diversity Awards. These awards recognize those who help foster a 
climate of inclusion and diversity that creates an optimal learning 
environment for the entire school community. This important work goes on 
throughout the district by our staff, parents, community members and 
students and we are so pleased to be able to honor these recipients tonight.  
This is the fourth year for the Spirit of Diversity Awards, which were 
designed by the Troy Diversity & Inclusion Council as a way to honor those 
who go above and beyond to ensure that every student is valued and 
diversity is celebrated within the Troy School District. When your name is 
read, please come up to receive your certificate and nomination packet.  
Recommended Action:  Kerry Birmingham will read names of the 
winners and the Board will present certificates. 
Morse Diversity and Inclusion Team — Ashourina Yousif, Steph anie 
Zendler, Missy Batts, Jen Bell, Jen Reis, Jen Hilzinger, Meghan Burcham, 
Erin Keyser, Jacklyn Morrison. This group of individuals took on the mission 
of combating hate and educating the community about refugees looking for 
asylum and a new life in the US. They learned about the refugee process in 
the US and in Troy and then worked to combat the stereotypes around this 
vulnerable population. Then they held a ‘Welcome Home’ potluck and the 
fundraiser to give a warm welcome and aid in a fresh start for three 
newcomer families in our area. They also brought the school together in 
the spirit of community and brotherhood, educating one another and 
sharing in a common goal. In addition, Ms. Bell started a program at Morse 
where she will be providing families in the community with potted 
vegetables to take home. Through this initiative, parents will be able to 
provide their families with fresh vegetables throughout the summer. Her 
plans take her all the way into next year where she hopes to create a school 
garden where students and families can pick fresh food to take home. 
Hannah Nagi— Ms. Nay-jee is the sponsor for the Social Justice Project at 
Troy Athens H igh School.  Every year, she  coordinates the Eth nic Fair at

Megan Lubaway, Sarah Bolton, Megan Cunningham— The ECP Tikes
