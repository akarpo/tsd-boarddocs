B. CONSIDERATION:   Schools Closed to Open Enrollment for  
       2013/2014 
Administration has reviewed the enrollment for elementary, middle, and high 
school attendance areas.  Our goal has been to maximize the enrollments at 
both Athens and Troy High School resulting in both schools being closed to 
open enrollment for the past several years. For the 2013/2014 school year, it 
is recommended that 25 spots be available for 9th grade students scheduled 
to go to Troy High School but wishing to attend Athens High School.  It is 
projected Troy High School will continue to maintain optimal enrollment and, 
due to some continued demographic shifts, it is appropriate to consider a 
limited num ber of open enrollments be made available for the 201 3/2014 
school year to Athens High School.  It is therefore recommended that Athens 
High and Troy High School be closed to open enrollment, with the exception 
of the 25  openings made available for incomin g ninth graders only, who live 
in the Troy High School attendance area but wish to attend Athens High 
School for the 201 3/2014 school year.  Due to limited space, the district will 
be accepting applications from February 6 – 22, 2013. 
RECOMMENDED ACTION: Adopt the following resolution. 
SUGGESTED RESOLUTION 
 Moved by: __________________________ 
 Supported by: __________________________ 
Be it therefore resolved that the Troy Board of Education adopts a resolution to 
close Athens High and Troy High School to open enrollment for the 201 3/2014 school 
year, with the exception of not more than 25 ninth grade students who reside in the 
Troy High School attendance area , but wish to attend Athens High School.  Students 
with siblings attending Athens Hi gh School and currently enrolled in grades 9 – 11 will 
be allowed to open enroll to Athens for the 2013/2014 school year.

 Center 
 RE: Purchase of Web Filter  
4420 Livernois 
Troy, MI 48098 
Due to ineffective Internet content filtering, challenging configuration procedures, and inadequate 
user support, Merri Lynn Colligan began research in the Fall of 2012 to replace the current web 
filtering product with one that would meet the needs of Troy Schools students and staff.  In addition to 
the filtering and configuration difficulties, the current web filter, Deep Nines, does not provide granular 
settings for groups of users or effective reporting of user activity. 
Merri Lynn arranged for trial testing of two web filtering products, one manufactured by Barracuda, 
the other manufactured by Phantom Technologies.  Each of these products were tested for a period 
of 30 days on the Troy Schools network.  The goal of the trial was to identify a product that would 
meet the needs of Troy students and staff, while addressing the shortcomings of the current Deep 
Nines web filter. 
At the conclusion of the testing period, I sought bids for the Barracuda and Phantom Technologies 
web filters.  Two bids were available for the Barracuda product and three bids were available for the 
Phantom Technologies product.  One of the Phantom Technologies bids is offered via an Oakland 
Schools cooperative purchasing agreement that represents a significantly discounted price. 
I analyzed the trial testing results and bid responses to determine the web filtering product that would 
best meet the needs of Troy Schools.  The Phantom Technologies iBoss 14500 web filter costs 
$6,964 more than the Barracuda 810 spread over the three-year subscription period, but the iBoss 
14500 web filter offers significant advantages over the Barracuda 810.  The advantages of the iBoss 
14500 include: 
 Redundant web filter in place, no failover to open Internet access 
 Redundant power supplies 
 Better reporting system to track issues with users 
 More granular control of YouTube EDU with teacher video libraries 
 Recommended by Oakland Schools and Troy Schools technology staff 
Recommendation: 
Based upon my analysis, The Technology Department recommends the purchase of the iBoss 14500 
web filter from Phantom Technologies in the amount of $44,768. The award includes a three (3) year 
enterprise subscription for 5,000 nodes. The funding for this purchase will be the General Fund 
budget.

ADVERTISING & DISTRIBUTION OF MATERIALS IN THE SCHOOLS 
K‐1300  
K‐1303  COMMERCIAL ADVERTISING 
  No  commercial  material  which  causes  substantial  and  material 
interference  and/or  disruption  with  normal  school  activities  may  be 
distributed,  posted,  or  displayed  on  or  within  any  district‐owned 
property  without  the  written  consent  of  superintendent  or  designee.  
The  display  of  material  on  personal  vehicles  while  parked  on  school 
property  during  school  hours  is  permitted,  provided  it  does  not  cause 
substantial  or  material  interference  and/or  disruption  with  normal 
school activities. 
Further,  absent  the  express  written  consent  of  the  superintendent  or 
his/her  designee,  all  school  personnel,  including  contracted  personnel 
assigned  to  the  school  district,  shall  be  restricted  from  utilizing  Troy 
School  District  resources,  technology,  including  District  e‐mail,  and 
student/parent contact information received while engaged in duties for 
the Troy School District for commercial purposes.
