The Oakland Schools Board of Education and I invite you to review the enclosed proposed Oakland 
Schools budget for FY 2020-21.  As you know, Oakland Schools is primarily funded by  property tax 
dollars, and we are projecting a growth factor in Oakland Schools’ property tax revenues of 3.25%.  Tax 
revenue projections are conservative compared to Oakland County taxable value increase projections as 
there is concern that a Headlee rollback of our operating millage is possible for FY 2020-21.  This occurs 
when the annual growth on existing property is greater than the rate of inflation as measured by the 
Consumer Price Index.   Additional information will be received by June 1 regarding taxable values and 
the millage reduction factor.   
Oakland Schools has developed the Literacy Essentials Oakland (LEO) initiative in response to declining 
grade students using a collaborative program with all 28 constituent districts.  The program focuses on 
professional learning for teachers and administrators, a literacy coach in -residence program, 
personalized action plans for each participating district and a viable literacy curriculum with a balanced 
assessment system. The program will be evaluated by an external evaluator throughout the duration of 
the project. More detailed information regarding the goals of each  year of the program can be found in 
the Enterprise Wide Executive Summary.    
Career and Technical Education 
To help align ourselves with the Michigan Department of Education’s Top 10 in 10 Years goals and 
strategies, we are dedicating additional resourc es to career and technical education.  In addition to 
providing significant resources to our four technical campuses, we included additional funds in the  
2020-21 budget for career program redesign and initial funding for a CTE mobile classroom which is 
intended to bring STEM activities to local districts beginning in 2020 -21.  In 2019- 20, we increased the 
CTE allocation to LEA’s by more than 25% and are continuing that level of funding in 2020- 21.  This will 
provide local districts with additional resources for their local CTE programs from elementary to high 
school.

Rationale 
This materials adoption is part of the Troy School District regular nine-year adoption cycle. The health 
education needs of students are constantly evolving based on the health-needs and concerns present in 
society.  It is important that we update and examine our curriculum regularly to ensure our students are 
getting the most current information, presented in an age appropriate way.  It has been many years 
since the last adoption of health materials, and the current textbooks are very outdated.  The health 
teacher team has been supplementing with free online resources along the way, but a common 
resource would help them ensure a guaranteed and viable curriculum for students. 
Process of Textbook Selection

the potential publishers, materials, and resources to be considered for adoption.  Also, included were an 
assistant principal and the director of teaching and learning.

Sexual Education Advisory Board Minutes 
Meeting Minutes  – April 30, 2020 at 6:30 PM 
In attendance:  Cherie Armstrong, Katie Austin, Lisa Danhoff, Christine DiPilato, Natalie Haezebrouck, Kristy Pierce 
Hall, Christina Harvey, Nicole Jenkins, Kelly Jones, Peg Kennedy, Andrea Lothian, Randy Maddock, Ann O’Neill, David

Kristy Hall facilitated the meeting and began with introductions.  She explained the goal of this meeting of the 
Sexual Education Advisory Board (SEAB) is to review and discuss the health materials recommended by the health 
teachers for use in the middle school and high school health classes.  The charge of the SEAB is to ensure the 
materials are in alignment with the values and policies of the Troy School District and the community, and includes 
appropriate content as required by the Michigan Department of Education. 
Christina Harvey, health curriculum consultant from Oakland Schools, explained health curriculum includes topics 
about puberty, adolescence, healthy relationships, positive communication skills, identifying risky behaviors, legal 
consequences of risky behaviors, and Michigan law regarding required content.  Michigan law requires instruction in 
communicable diseases, such as STDs, HIV/AIDS, and sex education that focuses on abstinence.  Additional required 
content includes instruction about age appropriate and medically accurate information about sex, healthy 
relationships, how to identify signs of abuse, refusal skills, how to resist pressure to engage in risky behavior, 
adoption services, and the law regarding sexual contact.  Christina also noted that school districts may not distribute 
contraception, may not teach about abortion as part of family planning, or refer students to abortion clinics on 
school grounds.  
Natalie Haezebrouck, director of the Department of Teaching and Learning in the Troy School District, explained the 
collaborative process by which the 7th grade and 9th grade health teachers reviewed materials from multiple 
publishers over the last several months.  The textbooks published by The Goodheart-Wilcox Company (G-W) were 
recommended by the textbook selection committee.  The materials have the most current content with what 
students experience, and the publisher provides continuous updates to the online content. 
7

Natalie also explained that parents can choose to opt out of having their student participate in specific chapters, 
units, or lessons involving sexual education topics.  Lisa Danhoff and Andrea Lothian explained that, in their 
experience, very few students opt out.  At Troy High, Lisa has only had eight students opt out over 18 years, and 
Athens has had similar numbers.  At Larson Middle School, Andrea has only had two or three students opt out 
during her teaching career. 
Andrea Lothian, health teacher at Larson Middle School, introduced the recommended G-W publication for middle 
school health classes, Comprehensive Health Skills for Middle School, 2nd Edition, copyright 2021, written by 
Catherine A. Sanderson, PhD, professor of psychology at Amherst College, and Mark Zelman, PhD, professor of 
biology at Aurora University.  Andrea explained that this publication feels new and up-to-date.  The main 
differences from the old textbook are in Unit 8 where sexuality, sexual orientation, and gender identity are 
discussed.  Andrea explained that they want to teach to all students and not to only a specific group.  Chapter 20 
covers responsible decisions and pregnancy prevention.  Students are taught abstinence, and the teachers always 
emphasize that abstinence is the best option.  However, the teachers believe it is essential to have the information 
available to the students.  The review committee had intense discussion regarding technology and the impact it has 
on where students acquire information.  Medically accurate information is important, and the teachers need to be 
able to provide healthy, accurate information to our students. 
Lisa Danhoff, health teacher at Troy High School, introduced the recommended G-W publication for high school 
health classes, Comprehensive Health Skills, 3rd Edition, copyright 2021, written by Catherine A. Sanderson, PhD, 
professor of psychology at Amherst College, and Mark Zelman, PhD, professor of biology at Aurora University.  Lisa 
explained the primary differences from the old textbook to the proposed textbook are in the sections covering teen 
pregnancy and parenthood (chapter 21).  The G-W information is explained in much more detail than other 
materials reviewed.  The chart listing methods of contraception is more comprehensive.  Emergency contraception 
is new in this book.  The subject is asked frequently in class.  Legal issues regarding fatherhood and custody options 
are included in this chapter.  Adoption is discussed.  Abortion is covered in the textbook, but it is not discussed in 
class.  The recommended textbook is factual and not trying to persuade the reader.  Chapter 23 of the G-W 
textbook has the most comprehensive and current information regarding understanding sexuality as compared to 
other materials reviewed.  Chapter 24 covers prevention of pregnancy.  Methods of contraception are explained in 
more detail.  Some terminology has changed regarding condoms.  For example, internal/external vs. female/male 
condoms.  Lisa created a detailed chart of updated information.

meeting may accept nominations for a vacancy from the floor only if no nominating petitions have been filed
for the vacancy.
