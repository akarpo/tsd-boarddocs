GENERAL FUND BUDGET ASSUMPTIONS    
• The projected blended student count of 12,740 is a slight increase from 2015/2016. The 
pupil membership formula changed in 2013/14 to use current fiscal year fall (90%) and 
spring (10%) counts.  Previously, the prior fiscal year spring count was used. 
signature would have a net impact increase our foundation allowance $60 per pupil to 
$9,015.  A majority of this increase will actually be received through a categorical because 
our increase in foundation allowance cannot exceed the rate of inflation.  
• MPSERS Categorical changes are expected to have no net impact on the bottom-line  
budget. 
• 0.75% increase in property tax values. 
Salary & Benefit Items 
• The retirement rate is budgeted with an increase from 36.31% to 36.64% on October 1, 
2016.  The UAAL rate is increasing from 10.53% to 11.70%. The effective retirement rate 
is decreasing 0.84% from 25.78% to 24.94%. The new higher rate will be offset by a 
State funding element called “UAAL Rate Stabilization”.   
• The FICA rate of 7.65% has not changed. 
• Employee benefit costs for health, dental, vision, life and disability are estimated based on 
the State mandated premium caps. The limits for 2016 equal the 2015 limits increased by 
2.5%.  
• We continue work to adjust Salary and Benefits based on information to date.  Overall, 
the assumption is a net increase of 3% in salaries.  The following is a status of the 
employee groups: 
• Teachers (TEA)  
Salaries and benefits reflect the ratified agreement between the TEA and the Board of 
Education. 
• Secretarial (TESA) 
Salaries will include elimination of the wage reduction rates from 15-16; a 6% on-
schedule increase for those employees that will not benefit from step advancement and 2 
full-step improvements for all eligible employees.

Fiscal Year 2016-17 Grants 
 
 
GENERAL FUND GRANTS 
 
Adult Education Civics ($18,000) – Federal funds to support projects that demonstrate 
effective and innovative practices in providing and increasing access to programs and services 
that integrate English literacy and civics education. 
 
Adult Education Family Literacy ($143,000) – Federal funds to meet the extemporaneous 
needs of the adult education community to upgrade their skills and competencies. 
 
IDEA-Flowthrough ($1,953,352) – Federal funds to provide  instructional services for 
students with learning disabilities, emotional impairments or other eligibility designations as 
outlined in the IEP. 
 
IDEA-Preschool ($58,996) – Federal funds to provide services for “at risk” 3 -6 year-old pre-
primary impaired children and their parents. 
 
Michigan School Readiness ($313,531) – State funded preschool program to serve “at risk” 
four year-old students and their parents. 
 
CTE Millage ($110,944) – County millage  funds to support career and technical education 
programs. 
 
Title I ($600,203) –  Federal funds to help disadvantaged students meet high standards by 
providing instructional services to eligible K-8 students in reading, writing, science, math and 
social studies; provides parental involvement support; and instructional support for homeless 
students who attend the Troy School District. 
 
Title II, Part A, Teacher/Principal Training ($268,756) – Federal funds to implement 
smaller class sizes in grades K-3 and to implement professional development opportunities in 
the district to support building level, district level and curricular student achievement goals. 
 
Title III, English Language Acquisition Program ($306,899) – Federal funds to support 
English language learners from Pre-Kindergarten through 12th grade. 
 
 
 
TROY CAREER CENTER GRANTS 
 
Workforce Investment Act ($2,103,221) – Federal funds  to provide employers and youth 
and adult job seekers with seamless, integrated access to all workforce development 
programs and services operated by the Oakland County Michigan Works! Service Centers.

A. CONSIDERATION:  Resolution Against Nonpublic School 
               Appropriations  
The Legislature has sent its School Aid Budget proposal to the Governor.   
In Section 152b of the budget, there is an allocation of $2.5  million for 
nonpublic schools.  The appropriation was described by the legislature as 
necessary to cover the costs of state mandates on nonpublic schools that 
were identified in a 2014 MDE report. However, a legal review of the 
language has determined tha t this type of appropriation would likely 
violate the Michigan Constitution which prohibits public resources for 
direct or indirect aid to any private, denominational or other nonpublic 
school. Local school boards and districts  are being asked to pass a 
resolution urging the Governor to veto Section 152b and the $2.5 million 
for nonpublic schools. 
RECOMMENDED ACTION:  Accept the suggested resolution below. 
SUGGESTED RESOLUTION 
Moved By: ______________________________ 
Supported By: ______________________________ 
TROY BOARD OF EDUCATION RESOLUTION URGING THE GOVERNOR 
TO VETO APPROPRIATION LEGISLATION FOR NONPUBLIC SCHOOLS 
WITHIN THE STATE OF MICHIGAN 
WHEREAS, the Michigan Legislature has proposed a School Aid Budget which 
allocates $2.5 million for nonpublic schools. 
WHEREAS these funds have been specifically allocated to nonpublic schools as 
“reimbursements” for state-mandated conduct. 
WHEREAS these funds could be utilized to “reimburse” nonpublic schools for 
such things as the costs associated with e mploying staff, including noncertified 
teachers, as well as the provision of mentor teachers, and the expenses 
associated with certain specific areas of instruction, such as government and 
history, among other things. 
269
WHEREAS, Article VIII Sec. 2 of the M ichigan Constitution specifically 
prohibits: 
- the use of public money to aid or maintain a nonpublic school, 
- the use of public money to support the attendance of any student at  
  a public school, and 
- the use of public money to employ an individual at a nonpublic school. 
WHEREAS this appropriation is likely a violation of the Michigan Constitution. 
WHEREAS public schools do not receive similar additional funds specifically 
allocated to reimburse those schools for the costs of these same basic purposes.  
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED  that the Troy Board of Education 
urges the Governor of the State of Michigan to VETO Section 152b of the 
Legislature’s proposed School Aid Budget and thereby uphold the Michigan 
Constitution as well as the will of the people, as demonstrated by multiple prior 
ballot initiatives to fund private schools with public funds, all of which were 
rejected by the citizens of this state.
