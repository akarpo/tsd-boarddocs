Temperature Monitoring System for Food Services 
November 2012 – Began the process of investigating a temperature monitoring 
system 
May 2014 – Installed a pilot unit with Smart Temps at Larson to see if it provided 
the service that we were looking for.  As of February, 2015, the system has been 
very dependable by giving alerts as necessary. 
Based on the information that was gathered, Troy understood that this 
continuous temperature monitoring system would not only monitor the various 
walk-ins, reach-ins, and hot holding units, it would also provide us with 
documentation of the various food temperatures during preparation and serving.  
Monitoring food temps during preparation & serving is mandatory with HACCP, 
and with a temperature monitoring system we could isolate the area of 
breakdown if one should occur.  Currently we are doing it manually, which could 
cause error, or in a rush, not get done at all. 
It was felt that a temperature monitoring system would provide the necessary 
documentation and reduce our risk against the liability in the event of a food 
borne illness breakout.

The following is a summary of the most recent and significant factors leading to the budget 
amendment that will be presented to the Board of Education at the February 10, 2015 Board of 
Education Workshop and recommended for approval at the March 3, 2015 Board of Education 
Meeting.  The budget is analyzed by revenue source and expenditure object code: 
Revenue 
 Local Revenue consists of funds received by the collection of non -homestead and hold -
harmless property tax valuations , gate receipts, interest revenue, and miscellaneous 
revenue.  The property tax collection is made by the City of Troy and distributed to the 
district in installments.  We are a split -levy which means we divide our millage levy into 
both summer and winter collections.  Local revenue has not been significantly impacted 
in this amendment.     
 State revenue is recommended to increase by 7.6% from the original adopted budget.  
This is primarily the result of an increase in the State ’s payment to Local Education 
Agencies (LEA’s) for the Michigan Public Schools Employee Retirement Systems 
(MPSERS) Unfunded Actuarial Accrued Liability (UAAL).  The rate increased from 
4.56% to 7.36% which equates to approximately $3.3 million in additional revenue.  The 
revenue is offset with a corresponding expenditure which will be explained later in the 
analysis of the  benefit expenditures.  The amendment also reflects anticipated State 
revenue as stated on our January State Aid Status Report.  Using these numbers in the 
amendment resulted in an additional $3.1 million increase in State revenue from our 
original adopted budget.     
 Federal revenue was increased  2% to recognize actual grant awards.  These revenues are 
offset dollar for dollar with expenditures.  Our federal programs include: Adult 
Education, IDEA (Special Education), IDEA Preschool, Title I ( Education for 
Disadvantaged), Title II (Professional Development), and Title III (Immigrant, English 
Second Language). 
 Incoming Transfers and Other Transactions accounted for the majority of the 8.7% total 
increase in revenues.  This is largely attributab le to th e closing of the Durant fund as  
directed by our auditors during the last fiscal year audit.  Closing the Durant fund resulted 
in an inflation of current year general fund revenues in the amount of $5,619,125 .  This 
revenue is offset by a correspond ing inflation of current year expenditures as will be 
explained later in the analysis of the outgoing transfers and others expenditures.  P.A.18 
funding is also accounted for in this source of revenue and has been slightly decreased in

Payments are ne t of tuition expenses owed by local districts for the education of our

Expenditures 
 Salaries were amended to reflect current staff salaries and contractual obligation to be 
paid to employees for the remainder of the fiscal year.  The amendment resulted in an 
estimated $2.05 million increase in salaries from actual expenditures last year.  We will 
continue to monitor salary costs and adjust as necessary in the final amendment in June. 
 Benefits include life, disability, health, dental, vision, retirement, employer paid social 
security, workers compensation, unemployment, cash in lieu of benefits , and boa rd paid 
tax-shelter annuity payments .  All line items have been adjusted to reflect actual to date 
expenditures plus anticipated expenses for t he remainder of the fiscal year.  The majority 
of the 13% increase in benefits is primarily attributable to the a ccounting of the State ’s 
payment to Local Education Agencies (LEA’s) for the Michigan Public Schools 
Employee Retirement Systems (MPSERS) Unfunded Actuarial Accrued Liability 
(UAAL).  The rate increased from 4.56% to 7.36% which equates to approximately $3 .3 
million in additional expenditures.  The expenditures are offset with corresponding 
revenue which was explained earlier in the analysis of State Revenues . An additional 
contributing factor was a 2.3% increase in the hard cap rate as established by P.A. 152.  
 Contracted/Purchased Services bottom line  expenditures were amended to reflect actual 
expenses and encumbrances .  Contracted/Purchased Services include 
PESG/ESR/ESS/Coach EZ Personnel (i.e. substitute teachers, coaches,  maintenance, 
aides, etc.), Enviro-Clean Services (Custodial and Grounds), and First Student 
Transportation Services.  We will monitor these lines as we prepare for the final budget 
and amend them to reflect actual expenses.  
 Supply lines decreased in this amendment.  Textbook lines were amended to reflect 
actual textbook purchases to date. By the final amendment, all purchase orders will have 
been completed and most invoices paid which will allow us to be very accurate on the 
supply line item budgets.   
 Capital Outlay increased to a ccount for Technology Readiness Infrastructure Grants 
(TRIG) money awarded in which expenditures must equal revenue. 
 Other Expenses were reduced by 104% in this amendment.  The major contributing factor 
to this decrease was the assumption in tax abatement /write-offs.  The original budget 
included a $1.5 million annual expense whereas this amendment reduces the expense to 
$300,000.  We receive monthly invoices from the county in which we owe for prior and 
current year tax tribunal settlements.  The expense to date is approximately $200,000.  To 
our knowledge, most tribunal cases have been settled and our expense for the remainder 
of the year should not exceed $100,000. We will continue to monitor this line item and 
adjust in accordingly in the final amendment.  
 Outgoing Transfers and Others were increased by 172%.  This is largely attributable to 
the closing of the Durant fund as directed by our auditors during the last fiscal year audit.

Closing the Durant fund resulted in an inflation of current year gene ral fund expenditures 
in the amount of $5,619,125.  This expenditure is offset by a corresponding inflation of 
the current year’s revenues as explained in the incoming transfers and other transactions 
analysis. 
In summary, revenues are increasing approximately 9%, expenses are increasing approximately 
8%, and the operating deficiency is reduced by 28% or $350,628 from the original adopted 
budget.  A final amendment will be prepared beginning now and presented and proposed in June.

B-2001 WORKSHOPS AND CONFERENCES 
Members are encouraged to attend educational workshops and 
conferences.  Any expenditure of district funds for members for 
out-of-state meetings must have prior approval by resolution.  
Members will be reimbursed for all educational, leadership, and 
developmental workshops, seminars and conferences within the 
state.  Members will also be reimbursed for appropriate 
educational materials.  Members will be reimbursed for expenses

MEMBER DEVELOPMENT OPPORTUNITIES    B-2000 
B-2002 COMPENSATION AND EXPENSES 
Rate of compensation for Board members shall be $100 per annum. 
Members on school district business will be reimbursed for actual 
and necessary expenses incurred in the discharge of official duties 
provided one or both of the following conditions are met: (1) the 
Board, by a majority vote of its members at an open meeting, 
approved reimbursement of the specific expense before the expense 
was incurred, or (2) the expense is consistent with a policy adopted 
by the Board establishing specific categories of reimbursable 
expenses, and the Board approves the reimbursement before it is 
actually paid.  Each member is responsible for submitting an 
itemized statement of reimbursable expenses to the assistant 
superintendent—business services for listing with the regular 
monthly bills.   
Adopted: January 16, 1979 
Revised: March 17, 1998

MEMBER DEVELOPMENT OPPORTUNITIES    B-2000 
B-2101 MEMBER CONFLICT OF INTEREST 
A member shall not have direct or indirect personal interest in any 
contract with the District and shall not perform any labor or furnish 
equipment or supplies. 
No member of a Board member’s immediate family (father, mother, 
husband, wife, son, daughter, sister, brother, father-in-law, mother-
in-law, son-in-law, daughter-in-law, sister-in-law, or brother-in-
law) shall be hired by the District in any capacity during the term 
of office of the member.  If a member of the immediate family of a 
newly elected Board member is already an employee of the District, 
such Board member shall abstain from voting on any matter 
affecting the employment status of the employee. 
Adopted: June 21, 1976 
Revised: January 16, 1979

   BOARD APPOINTMENT TO FILL VACANCY B-3000 
B-3001 BOARD APPOINTMENT TO FILL VACANCY 
 If less than a majority of vacancies occurs on the Board of 
Education, the Board will announce the vacancy(s) through 
available media, making the selection of a new member open to all 
registered voters of the Troy School District.  Applicants will 
submit a letter of interest which includes a résumé and listing of 
community and school involvement. 
 Board members will review the applications individually.  In a 
duly called public meeting, Board members will nominate 
applicants.  After Board members have had the opportunity to 
nominate, the president will declare nominations closed.  A 
resolution and second will be requested by the president.  A roll 
call vote will be taken; the first applicant who receives four votes 
will be appointed to fill the vacancy(s).  The vacancy(s) will be 
filled within thirty (30) days after it occurs.  If a majority of the 
offices are vacant at the same time, the remaining member or 
members of the Board shall immediately call a special election of 
the Troy School District to fill the vacancies. 
Adopted: March 17, 1998 
Revised: September 2003

   CABLE CASTING OF BOARD MEETING    B-4000 
B-4001 CABLE CASTING OF BOARD MEETINGS 
 When regular Board of Education meetings are taped by Troy School 
District personnel, they will appear on the Troy School District channel, 
unedited. 
 When public comments violate Regulation 9130, Open Meeting Act and 
Family Education Right to Privacy Act (FERPA), or are deemed 
inappropriate by the Board’s legal counsel, the portion will be deleted 
before the tape is cablecast, and the following disclaimer will appear: 
 It has been the practice of the Board of Education of the Troy School District to 
rebroadcast its meetings on the district’s cable channel to fully inform the public 
of Board action, even though there is no legal requirement to do so.  However, 
because of potential legal concerns arising from the public comments section of 
the ___________ meeting, and upon the advice of its legal counsel, the Board 
has decided not to rebroadcast that portion of the public comments section 
which commenced at approximately _______ and ended at approximately 
__________.
