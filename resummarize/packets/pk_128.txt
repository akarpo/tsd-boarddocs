A. Troy Foundation for Educational Excellence Mini-Grant Recipients 
 
This evening we are pleased to recognize the Troy Foundation for 
Educational Excellence and the work they do to support our schools, our 
students and our staff. The Foundation awards grants to help fund projects 
that support the curriculum in every school in the Troy School District.  
 
The Foundation is made up of volunteers who give of their time and talents 
to raise money and support innovative projects that enrich student learning.  
 
At this time, I would like to introduce  Julie McClure, President of the TFEE, 
who will briefly recognize the most recent group of grant recipients and the 
many diverse ways they are enhancing the education of our Troy School 
District students.

B. Mission Moment – Innovator of the Year Award; Larson Middle School 
        Students 
 
As this evening’s Mission Moment, we would like to take the opportunity to recognize 
Larson Middle  School for their Plastic Ocean Project.  Larson 8th grade students 
engaged in a year -long project that began by researching the worldwide issue of 
plastic contamination of our waters.   They were inspired to make changes in their 
own lives and at their school in an effort to reduce plastic waste.   They were assisted 
by Larson teachers Sandy Bammel, Shannon Dziwanowski, and Dave Ivanko, who 
were recognized as TFEE Innovators of the Year. 
 
RECOMMENDED ACTION: Invite Larson Principal Joe Duda up to  
introduce the teachers and students.

C. National School of Character Award—Larson Middle School  
 
This evening, we would also like to  honor Larson Middle School for receiving 
the 2019 National School of Character Award.  
 
Larson is only one of only 88 schools across the country to receive this 
important distinction from Character.org, a national advocate and leader for 
character in education. The program recognized Larson as a school that has 
demonstrated its use of character development to drive a positive imp act on 
academics, student behavior and Larson’s cultural climate. 
 
Under the leadership of Principal Joe Duda, the committee praised Larson for 
promoting good character, culture and ethics; fostering student self -
motivation, giving students the opportunity  for moral action and engaging 
families and community members as partners in character-building efforts.   
 
At this time, we would like to invite Mr. Duda up to receive a certificate from 
the Board of Education.    
 
 
Recommended Action:  Board presents certificate to Joe Duda.

2019-20 PROPOSED  
GENERAL FUND BUDGET ASSUMPTIONS    
 
• The projected blended student count of 13,050 is decrease of 46 students from the 2018-19 blended 
student count of 13,096. The pupil membership formula is 90% of current fiscal year fall count and 
10% previous fiscal years spring count. Our spring 2019 count is 13,061.16 which means our fall 
2019 count will need to be 13,048.76 [13,050 = (.9 x 13,048.76) + (.1 x 13,061.16)].   
 
• The projected FY 19-20 foundation allowance is $9,295 per pupil, which represents a $100 per pupil 
increase from FY 18-19.  At this time, a budget has not been finalized by the State.  The Governor’s 
budget proposal would provide Troy with a $180 per pupil increase from FY 18-19 as well as 
increases to certain categorical revenues, while the Senate’s proposal would provide the district with a 
$100 per pupil increase.  The House’s proposal is unknown at the time of our budget development.   
 
• Other Financing Sources Revenue includes a $1.1 million transfer of excess fund balance from the 
Adult Education and Community Service Fund.   
 
Salary & Benefit Items 
 
• Salary and benefit costs have been adjusted based on current information.  Overall, the assumption is 
a net increase of 1.0% in salaries or approximately $773,000, which considers contractual wage 
increase obligations effective as of July 1, 2019.  
 
• The retirement rate is budgeted with an increase from 38.39% to 39.91% effective October 1, 2019.  
The UAAL rate is increasing 0.20 basis points, or 1.6%, from 12.21% to 12.41%. The effective 
retirement rate is increasing 1.32 basis points or the equivalent of 5% from 26.18% to 27.50 %. The 
new higher rate will be offset by a State funding element called “UAAL Rate Stabilization.”  
 
• The FICA rate of 7.65% has not changed. 
 
• Employee benefit costs for health insurance are based on the State mandated premium caps. The 
limits for 2020 equal the 2019 limits increased by 1.0% (one-half the 2.0% increase effective January 
1, 2020). 
 
 
Other Non-salary Items   
 
• Increase in purchased services of 2.3% which equates to approximately $469,000.  
 
• Increase in supplies and materials of 5.9% or approximately $426,000 and accounts for 
elementary and secondary science textbook and materials adoptions.    
  
 
Millage Rates –  
 
• The General Fund homestead hold harmless millage rate will be established at 4.8836 mills.  
 
• The General Fund non-homestead millage rate will be 18.00 mills.   
 
• The debt millage rate will remain at 4.1000 mills. 
 
• The sinking fund millage rate will be 0.9857 mills.

Fiscal Year 2019-20 Grants 
 
 
GENERAL FUND GRANTS 
 
At-Risk – State funds to service “at risk” K-12 students for the purpose of reading and math 
proficiency and being career and college ready by high school graduation.   
 
Adult Education Civics – Federal funds to support projects that demonstrate effective and innovative 
practices in providing and increasing access to programs and services that integrate English literacy 
and civics education. 
 
Adult Education Family Literacy– Federal funds to meet the extemporaneous needs of the adult 
education community to upgrade their skills and competencies. 
 
Bilingual Education – State funds to support English language learners.  
 
CFE Millage– County millage funds to support career and technical education programs. 
 
Early Literacy – State funds supporting early literacy development.   
 
Great Start Readiness – State funded preschool program to serve “at risk” four-year-old students and 
their parents. 
 
IDEA-Flowthrough– Federal funds to provide instructional services for students with learning 
disabilities, emotional impairments or other eligibility designations as outlined in the IEP. 
 
IDEA-Preschool– Federal funds to provide services for “at risk” 3-6-year-old pre-primary impaired 
children and their parents. 
 
Title I– Federal funds to help disadvantaged students meet high standards by providing instructional 
services to eligible K-8 students in reading, writing, science, math and social studies; provides parental 
involvement support; and instructional support for homeless students who attend the Troy School 
District. 
 
Title II, Part A, Teacher/Principal Training– Federal funds to implement smaller class sizes in 
grades K-3 and to implement professional development opportunities in the district to support building 
level, district level and curricular student achievement goals. 
 
Title III, English Language Acquisition Program– Federal funds to support English language 
learners from Pre-Kindergarten through 12th grade. 
 
Title IV, Part A – Federal funds to provide all students with access to a well-rounded education and 
improved school conditions for student learning.   
 
 
TROY CAREER CENTER GRANTS 
 
Workforce Investment Act– Federal funds to provide employers and youth and adult job seekers 
with seamless, integrated access to all workforce development programs and services operated by the 
Oakland County Michigan Works! Service Centers.

  RE: 2019-2020 Millage Rates  
 
   DATE: May 31, 2019 
 
 
Taxable valuation amounts have been received from the Oakland County Equalization Department.  
Overall, the District’s taxable valuation on all property has increased by $ 188,487,760 or 4.83% from 
the previous year.  The increase in taxable valuation occurred in both the homestead property and the 
non-homestead property.  Taxable valuation amounts are important to the District as the hold-harmless 
and debt retirement revenue are all dependent upon taxable valuations.  The following is a summary of 
the proposed millage rates for 2019-20: 
 
Hold-Harmless Millage Rate 
In order to receive our entire per pupil foundation allowance, a hold-harmless millage is levied.  In our 
case, we are authorized to levy a millage that will generate $1,034.18 per student.  This millage is only 
levied on homestead property in the District.  The proposed hold-harmless millage rate for 2019-20 
is 4.8836 mills.  This amount is a decrease of .3484 mills from the current year millage rate of 5.2320.   
 
Non-Homestead Millage Rate 
All districts in the State have authority to levy 18.0000 mills, with voter approval, on all non-homestead 
properties.  The best example of non -homestead properties is the c ommercial and industrial real 
properties within the District.  Homeowners do not pay this tax on their principal residence.  The tax 
revenue from this levy is a significant part of the per pupil foundation allowance that we are able to 
generate.  The proposed non-homestead millage rate for 2019-20 is 18.00 mills. 
 
Commercial Personal Property Millage Rate 
Beginning in 2008-09, Commercial Personal Property is classified as homestead property for Headlee 
rollback calculation purposes and receives an exemption  from the first 12 mills of non -homestead 
operating millage.  This 12 mill exemption is reduced by the hold -harmless millage levied by the 
District.  As a result, a separate millage rate of  10.8836 will be levied on Commercial Personal 
Property in 2019-20. 
 
Industrial Personal Property Millage Rate 
Beginning in 2008-09, Industrial Personal Property is classified as homestead property and receives an 
exemption from the entire 18 mills of non -homestead operating millage.  This 18 mill exemption is 
reduced by the hold-harmless millage levied by the District.  As a result, a millage rate of 4.8836 will 
be levied on Indu strial Personal Property in 201 9-20.  This rate is identical to the hold -harmless 
millage rate levied on homeowners.

Debt Retirement Millage Rate 
The Debt Retirement Fund is used to account for the accumulation and use of assets for the payment 
of principal and interest on general long -term debt.  The primary source of revenue in this fund is 
generated through property taxes by levying a debt retire ment millage rate.  The proposed millage 
rate for 2019-20 will be 4.1000 mills, and represent no change  from the current year levy of 4. 1000 
mills.  The millage is necessary to pay the principal and interest on the bonded debt maturing in 2019-
20. The rev enue generated through this millage rate along with the fund balance in the debt retirement
fund will be sufficient to meet our debt obligation for the 2019-20 fiscal year.
Sinking Fund Millage Rate 
The Sinking Fund is used to account for the resources and expenditures associated with the 
communities approval of the building and site sinking fund millage in November 2017.  The funds are 
to be used to make infrastructure improvements and repairs to the  school district’s facilities.  The 
primary source of revenue in this fund is generated through property taxes by levying a building and 
site sinking fund millage rate.  The proposed millage rate for 2019 -20 will be .9857 mills, and 
represent a .0065 decre ase from the current year levy of .9922 mills.   The estimated sinking revenue 
for the 2019-20 school year is $4,033,588.
