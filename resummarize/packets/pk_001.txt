B. Michigan Association of School Personnel 
Administrators’ Award – Human Resources Department 
A Troy School District leadership development program has 
been honored at the statewide level for its creative and 
innovative approach in the area of human resources. 
The Troy Academy of Learning Leaders (TALL) earned the 2009 
“Best Practices in Human Resources” award from the Michigan 
Association of School Personnel Administrators (MASPA).  The 
award was presented to Jasen Witt, assistant superintendent 
for human resources at the MASPA annual conference in 
December. 
The TALL program, now in its second year, allows 14 current 
Troy School District teacher leaders to participate in monthly 
training sessions and mentoring that is facilitated by district 
administration, benefiting from program readings, and learning 
from real-life experiences through job shadowing opportunities.

A.  NASA Downlink Report 
On Monday, February 1, the Troy School District took center 
stage as one of only six districts in the nation to host a live 
downlink with crew members aboard the International Space 
Station.  
This very successful event took place at Athens High School 
and was the centerpiece of a series of science-related activities 
that involved more than 500 students in learning more about 
the space program and principles of flight.  The downlink was 
aired into classrooms across the district and was streamed live 
on the NASA Web site.  
The success of this program can be directly attributed to the 
guidance and determination of Jennifer Gottlieb, our science 
coordinator for the district and her team.  We would like to 
invite Mrs. Gottlieb to come forward at this time to share with 
the Board of Education and community the overwhelming 
success of this effort.

 B. REPORT:  Results of Parent Survey  
In order to fulfill District Accreditation responsibilities, the Troy 
School District conducted a parent survey from November 16 th  
to November 23rd, 2009.  With the help of Oakland Schools, the 
survey was administered online. The survey consisted of 82 
Likert style and 2 open-ended questions and took approximately 
fifteen minutes to complete.  Thirty-eight percent (or 2,752) of 
the surveys were completed.  (A common metric for sampling 
success is thirty-five percent.) 
In addition to the online format, paper copies were provided at 
all elementary schools and computer access was made available 
at the Services Building for those who did not have internet 
access from their home. 
The survey was intended to measure progress toward achieving 
district school improvement goals and to provide direction for 
future efforts.  While individual responses are confidential, the 
results will be used to address the needs of all students at the 
school and district levels.   
District and building results will be provided to all schools.

 C.   CONSIDERATION: Schools Closed to Open Enrollment  for 2010/2011 
 Administration has reviewed the enrollment for elementary, middle school, 
and high school attendance areas.  Our goal has been to maximize the 
enrollments at both Athens and Troy High School, resulting in both schools 
being closed to open enrollment for the past two years.  Troy High School 
has maintained optimal enrollment and, due to some une xpected 
demographic shifts, it is appropriate to consider a limited number of open 
enrollments be made available for the 2010-2011 school year at Athens High 
School.  It is therefore recommended that Athens High School and Troy 
High School be closed to open enrollment, with the exception that 25 
openings are made available for incoming ninth graders who live in the 
Troy High School attendance area but wish to attend Athens High School 
for the 2010-2011 school year.  Due to limited space, the district will be 
accepting applications from February 4-19, 2010.

Students with siblings attending Athens High School and currently enrolled in 
grades 9–11 will be allowed to open enroll to Athens for the 2010-2011 school year.
