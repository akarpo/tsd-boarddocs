1. What personal characteristics define an excellent administrator?
An excellent leader is often the determining factor within an organization. When an effort or team is headed by the right individual,
time, resources and skills are maximized and people are empowered, inspired and productive. In my experience, the three most
important personal characteristics of an administrator are humility, integrity and vision.
Shouldering the weighty task of educational leadership requires humility and a keen focus on service toward improving outcomes for
learners. To be effective, selflessness and a healthy appreciation for collaboration are essential. When administrators adopt attitudes of
servant leadership, educators and stakeholders sense their value and are driven to contribute toward common goals. Conversely, ego
driven leadership breeds competitive and hostile environments in which individuals fight for their own agendas rather than a shared
vision.
Integrity in administrators signals an alignment between the leader’s core beliefs and daily practice. Educational organizations have the
implicit mandate to care for and nurture learners in environments that foster curiosity and inquiry. However, whether that is evident in
its daily operations depends heavily on an administrator’s ability to consistently model and support those values. Said differently,
integrity in an educational leader translates to strong cultural values within the organization he or she leads.
A concise and clearly communicated vision provides stakeholders with a roadmap with which to navigate the nuanced and
ever-changing landscape of education. Even in the face of unforeseen circumstances and complicated details, organizations with a
shared vision can remain focused on priorities while marking progress along an established path forward. Leaders who provide vision
help prevent individuals and organizations from becoming sidetracked and discouraged by either the perception or reality of wasted
effort.
2. From your point of view, how important is technology in education? What technology-related skills can you contribute to a school
district?
21st century learners have at their disposal more information that any generation that came before them. The ever expanding digital
database of the internet can at times present as both gift and curse. The gift is evident: the opportunity to access worlds of knowledge at
the click of a mouse or tap of a screen vastly levels the playing field between educational haves and have nots. The curse however,
remains looming. How, within the sea of data are they to filter fact from fiction, and truth from lies? I posit that the answer to this
question, and the contributions I am most compelled to make in the area of technology are a healthy sense of perspective and an
emphasis on critical thinking.
As technology advances, it is essential that it remains a supplement to human understanding and connection and not a replacement for it.

Just as the pencil and printing press revolutionized their respective eras, the Information Age is dominated by rapid development and
increasing inter connectivity. Still, these advancement’s greatest potential remains grounded in their ability to facilitate human
connections and the exchange of disparate views. Learners can enter an increasingly diverse global community with the tools to both
express their own perspectives and understand those of individuals living on the opposite hemisphere.

increasingly necessary to balance present day wisdom with historical context. Further, the wealth of knowledge available includes voices

20-084













The Michigan Council of Teachers of English (MCTE) Diversity, Inclusion, Justice, and Equity (DIJE) subcommittee has awarded Jodie Duda, Erin Fischer-Ballew, Annie Gallagher, Christina Rick, and Valerie Valentino of Troy High School on their reception of a DIJE professional development grant.
The goal of the professional development grant is to support Michigan English teachers across the state as they engage in professional development that they design that privileges diversity, inclusion, justice, and equity work.  Jodie, Erin, Annie, Christina, and Val have created a program of reading and learning that supports their commitment to “disrupting stock stories, stereotypes, and westernized narratives.” Their selection of the following texts demonstrates their desire to learn more about themselves, their students, and how they can together pursue justice and activism: Black Appetite. White Food: Issues of Race, Voice and Justice Within and Beyond the Classroom, by Jamila Lyiscott; We Got This.: Equity, Access, and the Quest to Be Who Our Students Need Us to Be, by Cornelius Miner; The Anti-Racist Teacher: Reading Instruction Workbook, by Lorena German; Educating Activist Allies: Social Justice Pedagogy with the Suburban and Urban Elite by Katy Swalwell; and Linguistic Justice: Black Language, Literacy, Identity, and Pedagogy, by April Baker-Bell. Their intention is to use the concepts and practice presented in their readings to plan learning for their students to build community and engage in rich activist lives.

Governor Whitmer’s Executive Order 2020-142 provides a structure to support all schools in Michigan as they plan for a return of Pre-K - Post High School Education in the fall. Under this order, school districts in Michigan must adopt a COVID-19 Preparedness and Response Plan laying out how they will cope with the disease across the various phases of the Michigan Safe Start Plan.  

Each district must submit a reconfirmation plan to its Board of Education for approval on a monthly basis.  Once approved, each District must then post its Preparedness Plan on the district’s website home page for public access.   

At this time, the Board is being asked to approve the District’s Reconfirmation of the COVID-19 Preparedness & Response Plan.

It was moved by Dr. Philippart and supported by Mr. Hauff that

WHEREAS, Michigan Senate Bill 1108 of 2020 amended the State’s Open Meetings Act to restrict the reasons that a member of a public body in this State may attend a public meeting remotely beginning January 1, 2021; 

WHEREAS, advancing technology enabled school districts across the State to meet remotely during the COVID-19 pandemic, to develop and implement plans to educate our students safely and effectively, and to ensure safe and effective public participation in those meetings;

WHEREAS, the restrictions in Senate Bill 1108 allow remote attendance after December 31, 2021, only for absence due to military duty; 

WHEREAS, threats to public health and other disasters will not vanish on December 31, 2020, and contagious diseases, flooding, tornadoes, acts of terrorism or other causes occurring after that date could make it unsafe or impossible for public bodies to meet in person or for some members to attend in person;

WHEREAS, not allowing an immune-compromised member of a public body to participate in a meeting remotely would be a clear violation of the Reasonable Accommodation clause of the Federal American with Disabilities Act; and

WHEREAS, we believe that decisions about situations that may or may not merit remote attendance by members of a local public body are best determined by that body and the community it represents rather than the State Legislature;

NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED, that the Troy School District Board of Education:

Urges the Legislature to ideally, amend Senate Bill 1108, Sec, 3a,(b) to include a provision requiring local public bodies to set their own policies regarding remote meeting participation based on the needs and priorities of their own local communities rather than dictating universal policy to all public bodies in the State without regard to local control;
Urges the Legislature to, at a minimum, amend Senate Bill 1108, Sec, 3a.(b)to extend indefinitely its provisions allowing remote attendance to accommodate absence due to a medical condition or a statewide or local state of emergency or state of disaster, in addition to military service, and add accommodations for family emergencies and work obligations; and
Directs the Board Secretary to transmit copies of this resolution within five business days of adoption to Senator Mallory McMorrow, Representative Padma Kuppa, Oakland Schools Intermediate School District, and the Michigan Association of School Boards.
