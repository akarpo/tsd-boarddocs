A.  The Troy School District community was devastated when it was announced on 
October 19, 2009 that 20j funding was vetoed by Governor Granholm. This veto reduced 
funding to the Troy School District by $248.18 per pupil, or nearly $3 million annually. Our local 
lawmakers, State Sen. John Pappageorge and State Rep. Marty Knollenberg, have worked hard 
to fight for reinstatement of these funds. As lawmakers prepare for another year of school 
funding discussions, we felt this would be an appropriate time to share our appreciation with 
Sen. Pappageorge and Rep. Knollenberg for their efforts on behalf of our school community. We 
also urge them tonight to protect the financial future of our schools by continuing to work for 
the reinstatement of these critical resources.  
 
Resolution   
Recognition of State Senator John Pappageorge and  
State Representative Marty Knollenberg

Michigan school district; and

A.  REPORT:  English Textbook Recommendation – 
Grades 6-12 
 
During the spring of the 2008-2009 school year, English 
teachers in grades 6-12 began to meet to clarify what 
students need to know to be both college and workplace 
ready.  Teachers examined the college readiness 
standards and determined the learning progression that 
students need to follow to successfully enter the world of 
college and the world of work.  This learning progression 
includes skills benchmarks for language conventions.  
After developing this clear understanding of what 
students need to know and be able to do, teachers began 
to review materials to determine those that best 
supported our intended learning targets for students.  
Representatives from each grade level and building met 
to review materials and a clear preference became 
apparent.  Budget restrictions have resulted in the 
proposed adoption being spread out over two years.  
Grades 9-12 will begin using the new materials in the 
2010-2011 school year.  Grades 6-8 will begin using the 
new materials in the 2011-2012 school year.  Upon 
approval, we will be ordering enough textbooks for each 
student.  In addition, technology resources for the home 
will be available on CD and/or online.

 B. REPORT:  Read 180 
 
READ 180  is a comprehensive reading intervention program 
proven to meet the needs of struggling readers. The 
program directly addresses individual needs through 
differentiated instruction, adaptive and instructional 
software, high-interest literature and non-fiction, as well as 
direct instruction in reading, writing, and vocabulary skills.  
The program has been used in our middle schools since the 
2007-2008 school year.  Retired Troy School District 
elementary principal and reading specialist, Jan Hagland, 
has been working as a coach for the program this year.  Our 
READ 180  teachers are Renee Anderson from Boulan Park 
Middle School, Lauryn Mordarski from Smith Middle 
School, Kathryn Taylor from Baker Middle School, and 
Wendy Wilcox-Noronha from Larson Middle School.  They 
are excited to share results and successes from the program.
