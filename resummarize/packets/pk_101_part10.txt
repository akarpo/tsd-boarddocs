have a very competitive contract based on per route pricing and believe we can negotiate a better deal 
for a two-year renewal than if we were to bid the services.

our competitive per route cost, the starting wage for bus drivers is low relative to neighboring districts. 
This is causing difficulty in staffing; therefore, we will be recommending a renewal contract that will 
increase the per route cost by 5% - 8% for the first year and 3% - 5% for the second year.  Again, given 
some comparative data on per route cost of other school districts in the county and the nation-wide bus 
driver shortage, we believe we will be better served by negotiating a renewal around those parameters. 
This increase will be reflected in the 2017-18 proposed budget.

School, and Troy Career and College High School.  The initial term of our contract was for the three-

ending on June 30, 2020.  Our current base contract cost is $300,500 and it is recommended the base 
contract for the three years be as follows:  $305,000; $310,000; and $315,000; respectively.   
Clean Ed – Clean Ed employs six (6) facility maintenance technicians responsible for providing 
supplemental support to our ski lled trades staff throughout the district. The in itial term of our

of these positions.

Survey Background
• Because of persistent funding gaps from the State, the Troy School District continues to seek 
other ways fund quality education in the District.  
• In 2013 voters overwhelmingly supported the District’s request for a 125 million dollar bond to 
help cover many expenses over the bond’s 10-year lifespan. The District structured that bond in 
a way that did not raise property tax rates, but keeping the bond tax-neutral also meant it 
wouldn’t cover everything the District knew it needed.  The Board of Education hoped state 
funding would increase sufficiently when the economy got better, but that hasn’t happened.  
• As a result, the District continues to struggle to pay for important things like preventative 
maintenance on its buildings.
• Recent legislative adjustments to appropriate uses for a sinking fund have now made this a 
potential avenue for covering some significant district costs, such as maintenance, facility 
improvements and construction, safety and security measures, and technology. 
32017 Sinking Fund Community Input Survey Report472
Survey Background
• Consequently, the Troy Board of Education is considering whether or not to place a sinking fund 
proposal on the election ballot this November.  
• The District recently retired some debt, so it could structure a new 1-mill sinking fund so that 
the total school millage rate in Troy would stay where it is, with no property tax increase from 
what homeowners pay today.  That 1-mill would provide the District with 3.6 million dollars per 
year for the next ten years.  The funds would be used to fill current holes in preventative 
maintenance, update classrooms and technology in a timelier manner, and cover any debt 
payments on new or existing buildings.
• Given ongoing needs, it would be appropriate for the district to ask for more than 1-mill—
especially because a school district can only pursue one sinking fund every ten years.
• As a result, the Board commissioned a public survey of stakeholders across the Troy community 
to determine the electorate’s willingness to support this form of funding the district.
