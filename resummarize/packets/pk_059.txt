Project Scope 
The Welcome Center/secure entrance concept begins with a visitor entering the main building entrance 
vestibule, through exterior doors open to permit vestibule-only access for shelter in inclement weather.  
The visitor sees that on one side or the other there is a glazed wall and door that leads from the vestibule 
directly to the main office.  The inner set of vestibule doors is locked to prevent any further passage into 
the school. 
The visitor communicates with the main office secretary from the vestibule over an audiovisual station 
where he/she is identified and permitted access to the main office through the locked vestibule/main 
office doorway. 
In the main office the visitor approaches the main office secretary where identification and sign-in are 
completed.  The visitor then enters the school through a separate door beyond the secure entrance.

Special Considerations 
Please note that there was only one bid received for the general trades category of work.  There were four 
contractors committed to bidding the project until the last days of the bidding period.  The three that 
dropped out cited inability to staff the project if awarded a contract.  Although typically we would not 
recommend making an award from a single bid, but in this case we do not see the situation improving.  
Most contractors have their work load set for the summer this late in the bidding season.  With the Bond 
Issue approved in November, the Architect/Engineer was not able to start design work, and produce bid 
packages for the January/February time frame we would like to see.  Moving forward, this would be our 
recommendation. 
Also be aware that the apparent low bidder for the electrical category of work, Ecker Mechanical, 
requested that their bid be withdrawn, claiming their bid was incomplete.  We recommend they be 
allowed to withdraw, and award to the second low bidder Metro Electric.  Attempting to recover damages 
from their Bid Bond would delay the project, and may only result in a recovery of approximately $10,000. 
Budget 
The budget for this work is $2,669,975 resulting in an overage of $418,140, which will be funded from 
variances in other packages. The end budget amount of $51,360 from the roofing project at Larson and 
Boulan Park Middle Schools can be applied at this point.  Moving forward, we intend to manage the 
over/unders to at least a net zero add to the bond issue. 
Included with this recommendation is a tabulation of all bids received.

Re: Bid Award Recommendation iPad cases 
After attending the most recent Michigan School Business Officials conference, I learned of 
a newly introduced iPad case (The Shield) from MaxCase which provides the same level of 
protection as the Gumdrop cases and adds two significant benefits.  
1. The case has two open slots in the back that can be used for barcode and ID labels to 
inventory and identify the case without turning it on.  This will help the teachers immensely 
when trying to figure out who an iPad belongs to. 
2. The case is only $23 per unit instead of $30/unit; a savings of $35,700. 
The Gumdrop cases were selected off of the National Joint Purchasing Association (NJPA) 
bid vehicle.  Gumdrop has not been notified of the award so there are no vendor issues with 
this decision.  All of the cases are in stock from the company, MaxCase of California.  The 
MaxCases are also available via the NJPA bid vehicle as well. 
Lansing Public Schools has used an earlier model for the past two years and have had great success.  
One of MaxCases other products (a less rugged version of what we are purchasing) was awarded by 
District Administration Magazine Readers Choice award as one of the Top 100 Products of 2013.  
I am recommending changing the Board of Education’s previous resolution of the purchase of the 
Gumdrop Tech Cases to be changed to the following. 
5100 iPad MaxCase Shield Cases from Maxcase @ $23/each for a total of $117,300 
All items recommended for purchase are being expensed from the 2014 Capital Projects Fund.

One individual has filed as a candidate for the one seat open on the Oakland Schools Board of
Education Biennial Election.
Mr. George Ehlert (6-year term)
Oakland Schools board members are elected by an electoral body composed of one (1)
person designated by the board of each constituent school district. The Revised School
Code prescribes that each constituent school district board must designate a
representative to this electoral body by adopting a resolution that will 1) designate the
person to vote in the election on behalf of the local district board, 2) direct the designee
which candidate the board supports for each position to be filled, 3) direct the designee
to vote for those individuals, at least on the first ballot taken by the electoral body. The
designated electors must cast their ballots  in person at the election scheduled to take
place between 5:30 – 7:30 PM on Monday, June 2, 2014  in Conference Room A of the
Oakland Schools building located at 2111 Pontiac Lake Road in Waterford.   The revised
school code prohibits the acceptance of absentee ballots.
According to the revised school code, districts shall consider the resolution at no less
than one (1) meeting before adopting the resolution. Districts have interpreted this
language differently and historically have used one of the following three options to
consider and adopt their resolutions:
Consider the resolution as a discussion item during an open meeting and adopt the
resolution as an action item during another open meeting, with each meeting
occurring on a separate date
Consider the resolution as a discussion item during an open meeting and adopt the
resolution as an action item during a second open meeting immediately following
the first open meeting
Consider the resolution as a discussion item and adopt the resolution as an action
item at the same open meeting (Local school districts selecting this option should
seek independent legal counsel confirming a singular open meeting to consider and
adopt a resolution is compliant with the revised school code).
According to the revised school code, districts must adopt their resolutions no earlier
than twenty-one (21) days prior to the election ( no earlier than May 12, 2014 ).
After your board has adopted its resolution as prescribed by the revised school
code, please fax (248.209.2206) a copy of the resolution to the Oakland Schools’
Superintendent’s office by the end of the business day on Friday, May 30, 2014 .
This will enable us to compile a list of district designees prior to the election date.
Attached is a copy of the Revised School Code, a sample resolution template to
designate your district’s representative and selection of candidates, and an election
timeline.
An official notice of this election will be sent to your board of education secretary via
registered mail.
