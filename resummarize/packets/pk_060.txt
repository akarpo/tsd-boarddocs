GENERAL FUND BUDGET ASSUMPTIONS    
• The projected blended student count of 12,590.00 is a flat increase from 2013/14. The pupil 
membership formula changed in 2013/14 to use current fiscal year fall (90%) and spring (10%) 
counts.  Previously, the prior fiscal year spring count was used. 
• The foundation allowance of $8,885.00 per pupil for 2014/15 assumes $50 per pupil increase.  
• The district also included $50 per pupil for “Best Practices” funding as well as $100 per pupil for 
“performance funding” categorical funding. 
Salary & Benefit Items 
• The retirement rate is budgeted with an increase from 29.35% to 30.34% on October 1, 2013.  The 
effective retirement rate budgeted is 25.78%. The new higher rate will be offset by a State funding 
element called “UAAL Rate Stabilization”.   
• The FICA rate of 7.65% has not changed. 
• Employee benefit costs for health, dental, vision, life and disability are estimated based on the State 
mandated premium caps.  
• From the amended budget of 2013/14, salary expenditures have decreased $1,367,905 in the 
General Fund. The majority of this is attributable to TEA salaries not including the one-time fy 13-
14 stipend of $2.4 million, the inclusion of the TEA two step increment costs in fy 14-15 and the 
salary differential of 21 TEA fte retiring. 
All three collective bargaining units Troy Education Association (TEA), Troy Educational 
Secretaries Association (TESA), and Troy Educational Suppor t Personnel Association (TESPA) 
have contracts through June 30, 2015. The salaries are reflective of the settlements. 
• Teachers (TEA)  
The salaries reflect a new “stretched schedule from 11 to 14 steps. The schedule also changes the 
amount of a step in each lane so that all steps in a lane are equal in amounts of increase. For the 
fy14-15, members moved up two (2) steps. Some were more than two steps to insure their salary 
on the new stretched schedule was not less than their fy13-14 salary. 
• Secretarial (TESA) 
There is a 0.00% salary increase along with no step increases for TESA employees included within 
this budget.  The salaries do reflect a 4% off-scheduled payment to be made during fy 14-15.

GENERAL FUND GRANTS 
 
Adult Education Civics ($20,000) – Federal funds to support projects that demonstrate 
effective and innovative practices in providing and increasing access to programs and services 
that integrate English literacy and civics education. 
 
Adult Education Family Literacy ($123,000) – Federal funds to meet the extemporaneous 
needs of the adult education faculty to upgrade their skills and competencies. 
 
IDEA-Flowthrough ($1,900,131) – Federal funds to provide the instructional services for 
students with learning disabilities, emotional impairments or other eligibility designations as 
outlined in the IEP. 
 
IDEA-Preschool ($66,656) – Federal funds to provide services for “at risk” 3 -6 year old 
preprimary impaired children and their parents. 
 
Michigan School Readiness ($161,568) – State funded preschool program to serve “at risk” 
four year old students and their parents. 
 
CTE Millage ($130,318) – County millage funds to support career and technical education 
programs. 
 
Title I ($735,518) –  Federal funds to help disadvantaged students meet high standards by 
providing instructional services to eligible K-8 students in reading, writing, science, math and 
social studies; provides parental involvement support; and instructional support for homeless 
students who attend the Troy School District. 
 
Title II, Part A, Teacher/Principal Training ($240,304) – Federal funds to implement 
smaller class sizes in grades K-3 and to implement professional development opportunities in 
the district to support building level, district level and curricular student achievement goals. 
 
Title III, English Language Acquisition Program ($404,216) – Federal funds to support 
English language learners from Pre-Kindergarten through 12th grade.

and adult job seekers with seamless, integrated access to all workforce development 
programs and services operated by the Oakland County Michigan Works! Service Centers.

Report on District Superintendent 
Search Process 
Dr.  Philippart  informed  the  audience  and  community 
that the Troy Board of Education has been searching for 
a  new  superintendent  to  succeed  Dr.  Barbara  Fowler, 
the Troy School District. The Board has been assisted in 
the search by the Michigan Association of School Boards 
(MASB).   First and second round public interviews were 
held  on  April  21st  and  April  29th  concluding  with  site 
visits  to  both  candidates’  districts  on  May  1st.   The 
finalists  who  are  being  considered  for  the  position  of 
Troy  School  District  superintendent  are  Dr.  Richard 
Machesky,  Assistant  Superintendent  Secondary 
Instruction  of  Troy  Schools  and  Dr.  Steve  Matthews, 
Superintendent of Novi Community Schools.   The Board 
also  met  last  evening  in  a  special  meeting  to  review 
candidate assessments and site visit reports.   President 
Philippart provided a background on the superintendent 
search process that took place over the last four months 
and  shared  that  site  visits  were  conducted  by  three 
members  of  the  Board  –  Ms.  Underwood,  Mr.  Schmidt 
and  Mrs.  Fleming.   Dr.  Philippart  then  invited  Board 
members  to  share  their  thoughts  and  perspectives  of 
the  candidates  selected  as  finalists  for  the 
superintendent position.

pronounced  his  support  of  Dr.  Rich  Machesky  as  the 
District’s next superintendent.

shared that she was in full support of Dr. Machesky.

shared that he was also in support of Dr. Rich Machesky 
for the next superintendent of Troy Schools.

candidates  and  then  shared  that  she  was  in  support  of

Re: Bid Award Recommendation Mobile Device Management Software 
In the Fall of 2014, the Troy School District will be adding over 5000 new iPads for staff and student use.  
To manage the software/application distribution and device access, the staff of the TRC has been evaluating 
different Mobile Device Management (MDM) tools.  After an internal review of seven MDM tools, I am 
pleased to recommend AirWatch Enterprise Mobile Device Management for Board of Education approval 
in the amount of $208,921.50. 
Airwatch is being recommended for its ability to best handle the following challenges:  
 Configure consistent policies, settings across virtually most mobile devices  
 Secure the distribution of in-house and purchased apps and manage updates 
 Manage faculty, staff and student mobile devices uniquely based on ownership 
 Secure access to school resources, including Email, VPN and Wi-Fi networks 
 Deliver school software, files and applications remotely and with or without an internet connection 
The purchase of this software will be from CDWG via their National Joint Purchasing Association (NJPA) 
bid vehicle.  The length of the contract is 3 years; discounted pricing indicated above includes a per year cost 
of $66,420 for the licensing and $9,661.50 for installation/training.  Pricing is discounted at 30% discount 
on the overall three year cost by purchasing the licenses all at once.   
All items recommended for purchase are being expensed from the General Fund. 
Should you have any questions, please feel free to contact me.
