A.  Spirit of Diversity Awards  
This evening we are pleased to recognize eleven groups and individuals 
for our Spirit of Diversity Awards. These awards recognize those who help 
foster a climate of inclusion and diversity that creates an optimal learning 
environment for the entire school community.  
This is the second year for the Spirit of Diversity Awards, which were 
designed by the Troy Diversity & Inclusion Council as a way to honor 
those who go above and beyond to ensure that every student is valued 
and diversity is celebrated within the Troy School District. We plan to 
continue these awards for years to come as a way to recognize the 
wonderful work that is done in our school community.  
We received many stellar nominations for these awards and would like to 
honor the following people. Please come forward as your name or the 
name of your organization is called. 
Spirit of Diversity Award 
Ruba Alexander, Nancy Hanoosh and Sue Younan—who are being 
recognized tonight for their work creating and sustaining the Giving Tree 
program at Troy Union, which works to help in the area of socio-economic 
diversity. The Giving Tree operates all year round and allows families with 
difficult finances to post items they need anonymously. Other families and 
teachers at school then purchase the items families have requested.  
The Baker Buddy Program—represented tonight by Kitty Schmidt, 
Laura Kemp and Kerry Daniels, as well as advisors Ping Bai, Kelly Chlebek, 
Zachary MacIntosh, Courtney Oudsema, and Suzy Powell and 8th grade 
mentors Mackenzie Bachert, Stephani Bell, Kloma Cardoza, Cade 
Chapman, Amy Bray-Cotton, Connor Fogo, Rachel Goodman, Evan Griffin,  
Arpitha Gowda, Deana Johnson, Tessa Kaminski, Nathan Li, Mick Nivens, 
Najieh Oweis, Dhara Patel, Liz Pop, Mary Sharaby, Evan Sketch, Sariah 
Stockdale and Katie Walter. The goal of the Baker Buddy program is to 
further develop a mentoring relationship between 6th grade students and 
3
8th grade students in which all students continue working on becoming an 
individual, standing up for others, and learning from others.    
Joe DeVault and the Boulan Park LINKS program. The LINKS 
Program, which Mr. DeVault developed, pairs 8th grade students with 
students in the Autism Spectrum Program to work on projects, activities, 
games and academic programs. The LINKS also accompany ASD students 
to their general education classes. The connections that occur with the 
ASD students and the LINKS is magical and a true link is formed for both 
groups of students. LINKS has truly changed the culture of Boulan Park 
Middle School. 
Lori Medina-Yashinski and Neil Yashinski—who are being recognized 
tonight for their efforts to promote the beautiful cultures that are 
represented at Leonard Elementary as well as to provide a warm 
environment to exchange experiences and grow in understanding of the 
diversity in our community. Through a parent group called Leonard 
Cultural Connections, Ms. Medina-Yashinki and Mr. Yashinski help families 
share their rich traditions and unique experiences with others.  
Stephanie Miller—who is being honored tonight for her work to create a 
culture of inclusion and appreciation as Principal of Morse Elementary. The 
parent who nominated Ms. Miller says “she fosters a climate where all 
kinds of diversity should be accepted, talked about and understood.  She 
applies this to not only differences in culture, but also in socioeconomic 
status, learning disability, and special needs (to name a few). She has 
created an environment where individuals can keep their identity and be 
part of the group as a whole.  Students and Staff are reminded by Ms. 
Miller that everyone is special in their own way and we should each 
embrace our differences and others differences to create an environment 
that we can all learn and coexist together.” 
Jesse Allgeier, Deb Sheldon, Hannah Nagi and Renee Jamil—who 
are being recognized tonight for their work on the Troy School District 
High School Consortium/Troy Social Justice Project. This group of 
educators volunteer to lead and facilitate a group of their high school 
students in a leading edge cadre of high school students from around the 
region, for the purpose of breaking stereotypes, cross regional boundaries 
often divided by race and class and come together throughout the school 
4
year with youth leaders  from other communities. They help students to 
participate in a series of four Youth Leadership Summits, each with a 
different theme and workshops focusing both on diversity & inclusion 
content as well as skills development. The difference they have made is 
tremendous. 
Honorable Mention 
Smith Middle School Science Teachers Tracey Kania and Duane 
Losey—who are being recognized for their work to ensure that every 
student can learn and thrive at Smith. They also strive to include lesson 
on cultural diversity within the science curriculum. 
Troy Union Teachers and Staff —who are being honored tonight for 
their efforts to ensure that every student is included and valued and 
embracing the diversity that makes up our community. 
Oakland ISD Social Justice Project and Dr. Jay Marks —who are 
being recognized tonight for their efforts to help our staff and students 
create the Troy Social Justice Project, as well as other programs 
supporting diversity in the Troy School District.  
Jen Hilzinger—who was also recognized last year but is being honored 
tonight for her many, many efforts in the area of diversity and inclusion in 
the Troy School District.
