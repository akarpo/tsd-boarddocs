A.  Spirit of Diversity Awards  
This evening we are pleased to recognize sixteen groups and individuals 
for our Spirit of Diversity Awards. These awards recognize those who help 
foster a climate of inclusion and diversity that creates an optimal learning 
environment for the entire school community.  
This is the first year for the Spirit of Diversity Awards, which were 
designed by the Troy Diversity Council as a way to h onor those who go 
above and beyond to ensure that every student is valued and diversity is 
celebrated within the Troy School District. We plan to continue these 
awards for years to come as a way to recognize the wonderful work that 
is done in our school community.  
We received many stellar nominations for these awards and would like to 
honor the following people. Please come forward as your name or the 
name of your organization is called. 
Spirit of Diversity Award 
Padma Kuppa, who is being recognized tonight for her continuous 
volunteer work on diversity issues in the Troy School District. Padma 
works to bridge gaps in our community and provide a voice for those who 
are not always heard. 
Rouba  Samman & Jen Hilzinger - who are being recognized tonight 
for the Schroeder World Tour, a wonderful program that exposes students 
to many other cultures through dance, music, food and fashion. 
Nadeen Brown, who is being recognized tonight for her work on behalf 
of students in the Troy School District, and especially her leadership as 
president of the Troy African American Parent Network, her compassion 
and support to minority students and their parents and her efforts to 
improve opportunities for all students through the ACT preparation 
course, Saturday tutoring program and other outreach programs.  
Tonia Romancheck, who is being recognized tonight for her efforts to 
ensure that students with special needs are included and valued in every 
part of the school community. Tonia organizes opportunities for general 
education students and moderately impaired students to socialize, become 
5
friends and realize that we are all more alike than we are different. 
TAAPN (Troy African American Parent Network), lead by Nadeen 
Brown, Aretha Bradley, Constance Dansby, Tesha Long, and Cathy 
Hodges. This group and these volunteers are being recognized for their 
work to increase cultural awareness and advocate for the academic 
success of African American students. Their many contributions to the 
Troy School Community include the Saturday Excel math series, tutoring 
programs, and support and mentoring for students and their families. The 
group’s motto is “Look for greatness in each child and you will find it.”  
Jen Hilzinger, who is being recognized tonight for her continual efforts 
to embrace and celebrate diversity in the Troy School District. Among 
other initiatives, Jen organized and developed Cultural Coffee hours where 
parents of different cultures can come together as well as Activity Days 
and Ice Cream Socials at Boulan Park Middle School, where students can 
share and celebrate as well. Jen truly embodies the spirit of inclusion in 
every aspect of her life and was one of the founding members of the 
Diversity Council. 
Troy High School Head Peer Mediators—students Nico Adamski, 
Nicholas Arsanos, Connor Hayden, Hope Keating, Justin Losey, Miranda 
Mio, Thomas O’Donovan, Alexsys Pullen, Rebecca Ress, Rachel Sacco and 
Eve Theoharis, led by teacher Gail Yax. The Head Peer Mediators are 
being recognized for their work to maintain a respectful culture at Troy 
High School that embraces diversity and empowers students to become 
part of the process. Throughout the year, this group trains more than 300 
students in areas such as cultural awareness, communications and 
problem solving. 
Bemis Cultural Coffee, led by Bemis PTO members Jeanette 
Tanafranca, Vicki Wind, Janice Girling, Mehrun-nisa Ahmad and Kendra 
Cooper, who are being recognized tonight. The Bemis Cultural Coffee 
mornings brings together parents of many different cultures to practice 
speaking English, share stories and cultural traditions and provide a 
welcoming place for new families. 
Jesse Allgeier, who is being recognized for her efforts to create and 
maintain a culture of inclusion at Troy High School. Jesse has been an 
integral part of the Troy Diversity Project for more than three years, 
working on many programs to help create a warm and inviting 
environment for all students.

B.  Oakland County Outstanding Teacher of the Year Awards 
The Troy School District has many outstanding teachers on its staff. This evening we 
are proud to recognize the accomplishments of ten  individuals who were nominated 
for the Troy School District Outstanding Teacher Award Program. 
The ten nominees represent exemplary educators at every level and were 
nominated by their peers, by parents and by former and current students, all of 
whom talked about the impact these teachers have on the lives of those around 
them. Their nomination letters described how the professionals we honor here 
tonight help students find their voices, their passion and their love for learning.  
Would you please come f orward as your name is called? (Distribute 
certificates/plants.) 
First, we honor seven outstanding nominees, who have touched the lives of 
countless students and families in the Troy School District.  We are grateful for their 
dedication and passion for student success. 
Nancy Haboush, 1st Grade teacher, Leonard Elementary 
Elizabeth Mayne, Kindergarten teacher, Hamilton Elementary 
Lynn Mui, 3rd Grade teacher, Wattles Elementary

Karen Muzljakovich, 3rd Grade teacher, Morse Elementary

C. Troy Foundation for Educational Excellence Mini-Grant Recipients 
This evening we are pleased to recognize the Troy Foundation for 
Educational Excellence and the work they do to support our schools, our 
students and our staff. The Foundation awards grants to help fund projects 
that support the curriculum in every school in the Troy S chool District. This 
year, the Foundation was able to fund  114 grants, which is a record 
number of grants in a single year. That kind of support is invaluable. 
The Foundation is made up of volunteers who give of their time and talents 
to raise money and s upport innovative projects that enrich student 
learning. Again, these projects take place in every school in our District and 
many would not be possible without the resources the Foundation 
provides. 
At this time, I would like to introduce  Al Robinson, President of the Troy 
Foundation for Educational Excellence, who will briefly  recognize the most 
recent group of grant recipients and the many diverse ways they are 
enhancing the education of our Troy School District students.
