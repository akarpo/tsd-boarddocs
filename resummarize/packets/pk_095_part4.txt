EMPLOYMENT DISPUTE RESOLUTION PLAN 
1. Purpose of the Plan 
The purpose of the Troy School District (the “District”) Employment Dispute Resolution 
Plan (the “EDR Plan”) shall be to provide for the fair, efficient, and timely resolution of  
all claims, disputes or controversies arising out of or relating to an application or 
candidacy for employment, employment and/or cessation of employment , and to provide 
an applicant or employee with a forum in which claims or disputes with the District may 
be resolved exclusively by final and binding arbitration rather than litigation. This EDR 
Plan does not restrict an applicant or employee from filing a claim or charge with any 
state, federal or other governmental administrative agency. Rather, this EDR Plan applies 
only to local, state or federal court proceedings. 
2. Individuals Subject to the Plan 
All applicants or candidates for employment , and all employees not represented by a 
bargaining unit shall be subject to the terms of this  EDR Plan. For purposes of this EDR 
Plan, the terms “employee” and “employees” refer to current employees, as well as 
applicants or candidates for employment. 
3. Disputes Subject to the Plan 
Any controversy or claim arising out of or relating to a covered employee’s employment 
or a candidate or applicant’s application for employment, or employment contract, shall 
be settled by arbitration administered by the American Arbitration Association  (“AAA”). 
Michigan’s Uniform Arbitration Act (“UAA”), MCL 691.1681 et seq ., and AAA  
Employment Arbitration Rules and Mediation Procedures  (“AAA Employment Rules”) 
shall govern such arbitrations . Judgment upon the award rendered by the arbitrator(s) 
may be entered in any court of competent jurisdiction.  
a. Collective Actions Waiver 
Employees must individually arbitrate employment disputes except where 
collective action is expressly permitted by federal law. 
4. Initiation of a Claim 
a. Statute of Limitations 
An employee wishing to initiate arbitration of an employment dispute pursuant to this 
EDR Plan must file a Demand for Arbitration on the AAA’s website or with the AAA 
Detroit Regional Office, located at 39555 Orchard Hill Place, Suite 600, Novi, M ichigan 
48375. Demands for A rbitration must be filed within the time limit established by the 
applicable statute of limitations if the dispute involves statutory rights . If no statutory

rights are involved, the time limit for filing claims shall be thirty ( 30) calendar days from 
the date that mediation was terminated pursuant to AAA National Rules for the 
Resolution of Employment Disputes or thirty (30) calendar days from the date the dispute 
arose or the employee knew, or should have known that the dispute arose. 
Nothing in this EDR Plan prohibits an employee from filing a complaint(s) with a 
federal, state or other governmental administrative agency. 
5. Notice of Plan Implementation 
Employees shall be notified at least thirty (30) calendar days prior to implementation of 
the EDR Plan. 
6. Cost-Sharing of Arbitration Fees 
a. Filing Fee 
Employees shall be required to pay fifty percent (50%) of the arbitration filing 
fee.  
b. Financial Hardship 
In cases where the employee is able to demonstrate financial hardship and an 
inability to pay the employee’s portion of the filing fee, the arbitrator shall have 
the authority to order the District to reimburse the employee for all or a portion of 
the employee’s share of the arbitration filing fee. 
c. Hearing Fees 
Hearing fees shall be paid in accordance with the AAA Employment Rules. 
d. Hearing Room Rental  
The hearing fees described above do not cover the rental of hearing rooms. The 
AAA maintains hearing rooms in most offices for the convenience of the parties. 
Hearing room rental fees will be borne by the employer.  
e. Abeyance Fee  
Parties on cases held in abeyance for one year will be assessed an annual 
abeyance fee of $300. If a party refuses to pay the assessed fee, the other party or 
parties may pay the entire fee on behalf of all parties, otherwise the matter will be 
administratively closed.  
2 
128
f. Expenses  
The costs relating to proof and witnesses produced at the direction of the 
arbitrator shall be shared equally by the employer and employee. All other 
expenses of the arbitrator , including required travel and other expenses, shall be 
borne by the employer.  
g. Award of Fees 
The arbitrator has the authority to award fees when the employee is the claimant 
party, when such an award is in accordance with applicable law and the interes ts 
of justice. 
7. Compensation of the Arbitrator 
Impartiality is best assumed by the parties sharing the fees and expenses of the mediator 
and arbitrator. In cases where the economic condition of a party does not permit equal 
sharing, the parties should make mutually acceptable arrangements to achieve that goal, if 
at all possible. In the absence of such an agreement, the arbitrator shall determine 
allocation of fees. The AAA shall facilitate the negotiation of the parties’ share of costs 
and collection of such fees so as to reduce the bias of potential disparate contributions 
and shall forward payment to the mediator and/or arbitrator without disclosing the 
parties’ respective share of the payment. 
8. Neutral  Employment Dispute Resolution Plan Administrator 
The AAA is designated to administer the external compone nt of this E DR Plan 
(mediation and arbitration processes) . The AAA Employment Rules shall govern the 
mediation and arbitration processes. 
9. Due Process Protocol 
This EDR Plan shall adhere to th ose due process safeguards required by the UAA, MCL 
691.1681 et seq., and, where a dispute or claim relates to an employee’s statutory rights, 
the safeguards developed by the AAA Task Force on Alternative Dispute Resolution. 
a. Representation 
Employees using the mediation and/or arbitration procedures of this EDR Plan 
shall have the right to be represented by a spokesperson of their own choosing . 
The employee may seek such representation through various organizations, 
including but not limited to, bar associations, legal service associations, civil 
rights organizations, trade unions, etc. 
3 
129
b. Fees for Representation 
The amount and method of payment for representation should be determined 
between the employee claimant and her/his representative . In cases where an 
employee is able to demonstrate financial hardship and an inability to pay fees for 
representation, the arbitrator shall have authority to provide for fee reimbursement 
where the employee is the prevailing party . The arbitrator shall have authority to 
provide for fee reimbursement in whole or in part in accordance wi th applicable 
law or in the interests of justice. 
c. Access to Information 
The parties shall have adequate but limited pre -trial discovery , as provided by 
MCL 691.1697. Necessary pre-hearing depositions consistent with the expedited 
nature of arbitration shall be available. 
d. Mediator and Arbitrator Qualification 
The roster of mediators and arbitrators shall contain representatives with training 
regarding statutes, including substantive, procedural and remedial issues to be 
confronted in statutory rights employment disputes, skills in conducting hearings 
and knowledge of the statutory environment in which statutory rights disputes 
arise. Nothing in this EDR Plan shall prohibit the parties from jointly selecting as 
mediator and/or arbitrator one in whom bot h parties have requisite trust . Disputes 
shall be mediated by a single mediator. Arbitrations shall be conducted by a single 
arbitrator.  
e. Conflicts of Interest 
The mediator and/or arbitrator for an employment dispute shall disclose any 
relationship which  might reasonably constitute or be perceived as a conflict of 
interest. The designated mediator and/or arbitrator shall sign an oath provided by 
AAA affirming the absence of such present or preexisting ties. 
10. Authority of Arbitrator 
The arbitrator shall b e bound by the Arbitration Agreement signed by the parties, applicable 
statutes and regulations, the UAA, and the AAA Employment Rules , with respect to the 
arbitrator’s authority to determine the time and place of the hearing, permit reasonable discovery, 
issue subpoenas, decide arbitrability issues, preserve order and privacy in the hearings, rule on 
evidentiary matters, determine the close of the hearing and procedures for post -hearing 
submissions, and issue an award resolving the submitted dispute.

11. Scope of Review 
Pursuant to Michigan’s UAA, MCL 691.1702, either party may move for an order 
confirming the arbitration award in a circuit court of competent jurisdiction. The 
arbitrator’s award shall be final and binding and subject to limited judicial review as 
provided in MCL 691.1703.
