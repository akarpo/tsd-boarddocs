C. REPORT:   NASA DIME Competition 
DIME is a NASA competitive program that invites high school teams to 
develop an experiment concept, prepare a proposal for an experiment, 
and submit the proposal to NASA.   A NASA panel selects four top -
ranked proposals nationally.  These four teams will then continue their 
experiment development and fabrication leading to operation in the 
NASA drop tower in March , 2011.  NASA provides an expense -paid trip 
for each selected team to attend DIME Drop Days in March , 2011, at 
NASA’s Glenn Research Center in Cleveland, Ohio.   During DIME Drop 
Days, the team representatives will conduct their experiment in the 
NASA 2.2 second drop tower, analyze their data, tour NASA facilities, 
and participate in workshops.

non-compliant methods were rejected.

GradeQuick Web two years ago, therefore the THiNQ  Ed solution does not meet District standards.  The 
next lowest bid from Edline is fully compliant with all bid  specifications. 
Based on this analysis, I recommend award of a one year contract with Edline in the amount of $29,436 to 
commence July 1, 2011 per E-Rate regulations.  The E-Rate timeframe requires forms awarding the bid to be 
completed now in order to meet eligibility rules for the federal discount.

From: Scott Bryan, Director of Technology  
Date: January 11, 2011 
Re: Bid 9699 PRI Local and Long Distance service  
In compliance with federal E-Rate bidding practices and in order to secure an estimated 38% discount on 
telephone services, we released a bid for PRI/long distance telephone service for the district on November 
11, 2010.  Bids were opened January 5, 2011.  Four bid packets were received; the packet from Cavalier 
Telecom included two different bid price responses.  The packet from PAETEC Fairpor t NY had conflicting 
data on the bid form vs. the backup documentation and was rejected after review with the vendor.  Therefore, 
four bid responses were considered:  Cavalier Telecom measured rate, Cavalier Telecom flat rate, PAETEC 
Tampa, and Telcom Worldwide. 
The bid responses include a fixed monthly charge for each circuit plus a variable usage rate per minute for 
calling patterns.  I have created an evaluation matrix that used actual numbers of phone calls and per minute 
usage for the district from October 2010.  Traditionally October calling is a high water mark for telephone 
usage, so it is a reasonable “worst case scenario” to project telephone expenses.  Based on this, the attached 
spreadsheet projects that the response from PAETEC Tampa is the “wo rst case” low bid response.  Please 
note that, based on the nature of the bid responses, lower usage, i.e., fewer local calls or less long distance 
minutes usage simply increases the value of the PAETEC solution over other options.  
There were some clerical errors in the PAETEC Tampa bid form – specifically, they entered the monthly 
cost for all 4 circuits instead of each individual circuit.  However, the backup documentation/sample contract 
provided in the bid response permitted for an easy evaluation and correction, so although the initial “as read” 
bid opening pricing appears that this bid is highest by several orders of magnitude, the error is easily 
understood using documentation provided within the bid response.  Due to the overall significant cost 
savings over the life of the contract, I recommend exercising the District’s right to waive minor irregularities 
in the bid process – the vendor has not changed their response in any way as a result of my review.  
Based on the above analysis, I recommend that  a three year contract for PRI, local and long distance 
telecommunications services be awarded to PAETEC Tampa, contract to commence July 1, 2011 per E -Rate 
rules.  The E-Rate timeframe requires forms awarding the bid to be completed now in order to meet 
eligibility rules for the federal discount.  
Please place this on the January 18, 2011 agenda.  Thank you for your attention to this matter.
