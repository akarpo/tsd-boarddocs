C. TSD Mission Moment – Martell Elementary Students 
Students and staff at Martell Elementary are being honored tonight for 
their successful implementation and integration of the Leader in Me 
Program and their development of a mission statement for the school. The 
Leader in Me is based on the “7 Habits of Highly Effective People,” by 
Stephen Covey. Students are taught leadership principles and strategies 
based on the 7 Habits. During the summer of 201 4, teachers conducted a 
book study on this program to determine its viability for our community. 
Team members spent countless hours writing multiple grants securing 
over $50,000 to implement Leader in Me. Over five days of training has 
taken place during t he summer and weekends to prepare teachers, 
support staff, parents, and PTO board members for the implementation of 
this initiative. 
Research indicates the Leader in Me program has positive effects in 
numerous areas including a student ’s self-confidence, responsibility, 
leadership, problem solving, and communication as well as cultivating a 
positive culture. At Martell, the entire community has embraced this 
initiative and results are already being seen. The Troy Board of Education 
is excited about this opportunity to develop and sharpen students’ abilities 
and tonight we are pleased to recognize the following people , after which 
Mr. Lesser will tell us a little more about the work that has taken place:   
RECOMMENDED ACTION:  Advisors and students will come up as their 
names are called to receive their certificates and then will give a short 
presentation on Leader in Me at Martell.

D. Principal Appreciation Month 
Throughout the State of Michigan, February has traditionally been celebrated as 
Principal Appreciation Month, in recognition of the outstanding work of our 
building-level educational leaders. In Troy, we are fortunate to have an 
exemplary group of principals dedicated to the success of every student in the 
District. This evening, we will pass a resolution to commend our elementary and 
secondary principals for their ex ceptional commitment to excellence in the Troy 
School District.  
WHEREAS, energetic and inspiring school leadership is essential if teachers, 
schools and districts are to ensure that all Troy students are college  and career 
ready; and, 
WHEREAS, K-12 school principals play a vital role in the success of students 
and act as the liaison between the school and the community it serves, ensuring 
that parents and the community are aware of student and school achievements; 
and, 
WHEREAS, Troy School District pr incipals are dedicated,  quality educational 
professionals committed to providing sound leadership and guidance to their 
staff and students; and,  
WHEREAS, during this month, we join with educators, parents and students 
throughout Michigan to raise awaren ess of the importance of educational 
leadership, and to recognize and thank all principals working in Troy for their 
exemplary service; 
NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED , that the Troy Board of Education 
officially recognizes February as Principal Appreciation Month in Troy and thanks 
our dedicated building leaders for all they do for our students and staff.

E.  Spirit of Diversity Awards  
The Board of Education is proud to recognize eight individuals for our 
Spirit of Diversity Awards.  These awards recognize those who help foster 
a climate of inclusion and diversity that creates an optimal learning 
environment for the entire school community.  
This is the third year for the Spirit of Diversity Awards, which were 
designed by the Troy Div ersity & Inclusion  Council as a way to honor 
those who go above and beyond to ensure that every student is valued 
and diversity is celebrated within the Troy School District. When your 
name is read, please come up to receive your certificate and nomination  
packet.  
Recommended Action:  Kerry Birmingham will read names of the 
winners and the Board will present certificates. 
Nancy Lining —who is being recognized tonight for he r work to create 
and grow a climate of inclusion at Larson Middle School. She serves as the 
chair of the Larson Strong initiative, a character  education program that 
gives students positive messages and life lessons, such as standing up for 
one another and showing resilience. Nancy also led a group of 14 Larson 
students through  the Religious Diversity J ourneys Program, which is 
designed to promote greater understanding, awareness and knowledge of 
the many religions in the metro -Detroit area and prepare students to live 
in an increasingly diverse society.  
Dawn Hayes—who is bei ng honored tonight for her work in fostering a 
climate of inclusion, acceptance and value for all students at Athen s High 
School. She ensures that her room is a place where all students feel 
comfortable and often stop by throughout the school day. Dawn als o 
volunteers her time with the school’s Ethnic Fair, participates as an 
educator with the Troy Parent Advisory Committee’s roundtable 
discussions and facilitates an opportunity for Athens students to Shop with 
a Hero during the holidays. This year, she pla ns to launch a program to 
collect gently used clothing in her classroom so that economically 
10
disadvantaged students can choose what they need in a safe and 
nurturing environment. 
Jen Hilzinger —who is being recognized tonight for her work in three 
different TSD schools: Schroeder, Boulan Park Middle School and Troy 
High School. Jen is a parent volunteer who has served as co -coordinator 
of the Diversity Council, but also lends her time and talents to many 
individual diversity efforts at our schools. She coordinates the Schroeder 
World Tour, which celebrates the cultures and ethnicities of our students 
and staff. At Boulan Park, Jen created and headed the Parent Network, a 
targeted volunteer effort to reach out to ESL parents and get them 
“plugged in” to their  child’s school life. At Troy High, she has been a 
leading voice on many fronts, a strong supporter of the Social Justice 
Project and the coordinator of Fred Korematsu Day, which h onors the 
efforts to fight against Japanese internment during World War II. 
Kerry Brennan—who is being honored tonight for her work in teaching 
compassion and value for all to her students at  Wattles Elementary. Kerry 
uses the book Wonder, which chronicles the life of a boy with facial 
deformities, to teach her fifth grade students the importance of looking at 
what’s inside a person as the true measure of their character. When the 
class had finished the book, Kerry invited a young student with similar 
facial issues to speak with the class. This visit —along with the thoughtfu l 
discussions that took place in the class during this time —created a climate 
of inclusion and caring. Kerry was nominated by a parent who said she 
saw her own son grow a heightened awareness and compassion through 
this process. 
Lori Medina-Yashinsky—who is being recognized tonight for her work 
in organizing and leading a group called the Cultural Connection at 
Leonard Elementary School . Under her direction, this group has set up 
activities that celebrate the rich ethnic diversity of the school. As a paren t 
volunteer, Lori has also organized a cultural family picnic at Boulan Park 
and this year is spearheading a two day event at Leonard where students 
will be able to wear customary clothing from their ethnic background and 
teach others about the history and customs that country represents.   
Lisa Carruthers—who is being honored tonight for her creation of the 
peer-to-peer program at Leonard Elementary called “Leopard Links.” This 
program has created an environment of inclusion not just for students 
with Auti sm, which was the original intention, but for ALL students at 
11
Leonard. Lisa attended training and dedicated many hours of her own 
time designing and implementing this program, which has been a huge 
success. All students have learned tolerance and empathy a nd it can be 
seen in many ways every day at Leonard. Lisa  was nominated by the 
parent of a student with ASD who says that she—and this program—made 
all the difference for her son in school.  
Stephanie Sacco —who is being recognized tonight for her work to 
promote diversity and inclusion at three schools: Leonard Elementary, 
Smith Middle School and Troy High School . As the cheer coach at Smith 
Middle School, Stephanie continually teaches empathy, compassion and 
inclusion of all students. She has organized fr ee tumbling classes for 
students who could not afford lessons, mentored students who needed a 
place to belong and created a cohesive team and family atmosphere with 
33 girls from different races, ethnicities and economic backgrounds. 
Through her example, t he young athletes are learning values that go far 
beyond cheer. Stephanie is also a frequent volunteer in classrooms at 
Leonard and lends her time to the Troy High Boosters club so that all 
students have the opportunity to participate in activities. 
Nadeen Brown—who is being honored tonight for her work at Leonard 
Elementary School, but also for her tireless efforts on behalf of diversity 
throughout the Troy School District. Nadeen works with the staff at 
Leonard to help them learn to interact with new and  existing families on a 
cultural level. She also presents at new teacher orientation each year, 
guiding our newest teachers on the importance of understanding our 
students’ cultural backgrounds. One of Nadeen’s greatest contributions to 
the district is her  leadership, development and management of the Troy 
African American Parent Network, which offers free Saturday tutoring to 
students and many opportunities for families to connect. Nadeen was 
nominated by her fellow teachers at Leonard, who say she has a h uge 
impact on students, staff and our community. 
On behalf of the Board of Education, we thank all of these wonderful 
Diversity Champions for their wonderful work in our TSD community.
