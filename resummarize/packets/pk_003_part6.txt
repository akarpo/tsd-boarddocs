MEMORANDUM  
 
To: Mike Adamczyk, Assistant Superintendent, Business Services 
From: Scott Bryan, Director of Technology 
Date: March 16, 2010 
Re: Anti-Virus software license purchase 
Cc: Frank Lams, Purchasing Supervisor 
 
On March 1, 2010, the Technology Department issued a bid for financing the renewal of software licenses 
for Trend Micro anti-virus products for five years, to protect district desktops, servers, storage, and email 
systems.  Bids were opened on March 15. 
 
Three bids were received, with two being identically priced at $119,273.85 for the five year term.  Upon 
negotiation with both low bid vendors, a further discount was extended to the District to purchase outright 
instead of financing.  An equal 10% discount was offered by each of the low bidders, for a total of 
$107,346.47.  As Frank Lams and I discussed with you, the District is permitted by law to consider 
companies with headquarters in Michigan as a determining factor for selecting a bidder.  Based on this and 
on positive reference checks of the bidder, I recommend that this project be awarded to Networks Group, 
Inc., in the amount of $107,346.47.  I have attached a copy of their updated offer to this memo. 
 
This item requires Board action due to the dollar amount of the purchase; please place this on the March 16, 
2010 agenda.  Thank you for your attention to this matter. 
Barbara A. Fowler, Ph.D., Superintendent of Schools

Self -Monitoring   
Students will… 
 
1.  Track print with eyes and use finger only at point of difficulty, left to 
right. 
 
2.  Know a core of high-frequency words (18-30) that can be read 
automatically and can be used to self-monitor, using word parts and letter-
sound information. 
 
3.  Read familiar texts with fluency, phrasing and at least 98% accuracy. 
 
 
4.  Monitor for all sources of information during reading:  checking to make 
sure what has been read makes sense, sounds right and looks right. 
 
 
5.  Reread to problem solve, correct, or confirm. 
 
 
 
6.  Reread to search for information. 
 
7.  Recognize and use word parts (onsets and rimes) to solve words while 
reading 
 
 
 
 
 
“I Can” Statements  
 
 
1.  I can follow the words with my eyes and use my finger 
when I get to a word I don’t know. 
 
2.  I can read many high-frequency words quickly and read a 
new word by pointing and sliding my index finger through 
the word blending the sounds of the letters I know. 
 
3.  I can read familiar books like I’m talking, with good 
phrasing and fluency. 
 
4.  I can monitor my reading to make sure what I have read 
makes sense, sounds right, and looks right. 
 
 
5.  I can recognize an error I make when I am reading and I 
go back, slow down, and correct my error so that it 
makes sense.  
 
6.  I can reread to search for information. 
 
7.  I can recognize and use word families to decode unknown 
words.

bay) homographs (look the same, sound different: contract, contract), 
and homophones (sound the same, look different: flair, flare).

because they have the same base or root word (monarch, monarchs, 
monarchy). (RWS.05.02)

(ic(k) in dicker, organic; -ble in implausible, stable).

including vowel patterns with r, that appear in multi-syllable words.

MEETINGS OF THE BOARD     B-1200  
 
 
B-1211 PUBLIC PARTICIPATION IN BOARD OF EDUCATION 
MEETINGS 
 
 
All meetings, except closed sessions, are open to the public.  The Board 
welcomes citizens’ comments.  The public participation portion of the 
meeting is limited to one -half hour , but exceptions may be made at the 
discretion of the Board president.  Each p erson will be allowed to 
speak for up to five minutes, except where the number of speakers 
exceeds the time limit.  In those instances, the President may reduce 
the five -minute limit to a three -minute limit for each speaker  or the 
president of the Board ma y waive the half –hour time limit.  In any 
event, no one’s right to address the Board will be denied. 
 
Each person wishing to address the Board of Education must identify 
himself or herself by name and address.  If the person is representing 
an organizatio n or group, the person should indicate whether the 
comments or presentation represents the official view of the 
organization or group. 
 
Board of Education members may question speakers but are not 
obligated to answer questions or make statements or commitm ents in 
response to issues raised by the public.  In general, such issues will be 
referred to the Superintendent for investigation, study, and 
recommendation or designated as future agenda items for Board of 
Education consideration. 
 
Individuals addressing 
 the Board should take into consideration the 
rules of common courtesy.  The public participation portion of the 
meeting cannot be used to make personal attacks against any 
individual school board member or school district employee that are 
totally unrelat ed to the manner in which the member or employee 
performs his or her duties. 
 
To ensure individual due process rights, the District maintains a 
formal process for handling complaints against individuals.  A 
problem involving an individual or specific incid ent is handled 
through administrative channels.  For assistance, contact the 
Superintendent’s office. 
 
The president or designee is responsible for the orderly conduct of the 
meeting and shall rule on such matters as to the appropriateness of the 
subject b eing presented.

MEETINGS OF THE BOARD     B-1200  
 
 
B-1211 PUBLIC PARTICIPATION IN BOARD OF EDUCATION 
MEETINGS 
 
 
The Board values public participation and recognizes the importance 
of public input on issues affecting the Board and the District.  This 
Policy has been adopted to balance the importance of pub lic 
participation with other important objectives, including: insuring all 
community members a fair and reasonable opportunity to express 
their views to the Board; completing Board business effectively, 
efficiently and in an orderly manner; respecting other important time 
commitments of Board members, District staff and audience members; 
and, respecting the rights of Board members, District staff and 
students and audience members. 
Public participation will be limited to 60 minutes per Board meeting.  
Generally, there will be 30 minutes of public participation at the 
beginning of the meeting, limited to agenda items.  There will be an 
additional 30 minutes of public participation at the end of the meeting, 
limited to non-agenda items. 
Although the public participation portion of the meeting is limited, an 
exception may be made, if necessary, so that no one’s right to address 
the Board will be denied. 
Audience members who wish to address the Board must complete a 
Speaker Request Form.  Forms will be available 30 minutes prior to the 
start of the meeting and must be completed and submitted to the 
Secretary of the Board of Education before the start of the meeting.  
The Board President or presiding officer will recognize audience 
members for public participation subject to the following rules: 
• Each person wishing to speak must identify self by name and 
address. 
• Recognized speakers may be permitted to speak for up to three (3) 
minutes.  However, the President of the Board may reduce the time 
limit for each speaker in order to accommodate all of the 
individuals who wish to address the Board of Education. 
• Recognized speakers may not transfer or delegate their time to 
others.

C-1402 SMOKING 
 
The policy of the Troy School District is to follow Public Act 140 of 
1993 regarding smoking and use of tobacco products on school 
district property, with the exception that smoking is prohibited at 
ath letic fields. 
 
It is the policy of the Troy School District that the use and/or sale of 
any tobacco products is prohibited in vehicles, buildings, facilities, 
structures, or other real estate owned, leased or otherwise 
controlled by the Troy School District at all times (24 hours a day – 
7 days a week.) 
 
For the purpose of this policy, the use and/or sale of a tobacco 
product is defined as follows: 
 
“Tobacco product” means a preparation of tobacco to be inhaled, 
chewed, or placed in a person’s mouth. 
 
“Use of a tobacco product” means any of the following: 
 
/head2right The carrying by a person of a lighted cigar, cigarette, pipe, or 
 other lighted smoking device. 
 
/head2right The inhaling or chewing of a tobacco product. 
 
/head2right The placing of a tobacco product within a person’s mouth.

categories: 
 
/head2right Land 
/head2right Buildings

K-1303 POLITICAL 
AND/OR COMMERCIAL  ADVERTISING BY 
DISTRICT EMPLOYEES  
 
 No material supporting any candidate, candidates, slate of candidates, 
political issues, issues which cause substantial and material interference 
and/or disruption with normal school activities , or political 
organizations/committees may be distributed, posted, or displayed on 
any district-owned property. 
 
/head2right Except for the reserved length of time in a building or room which 
has been rented or reserved by a political or community group for 
meeting purposes or in connection with a class project or activities. 
 
All school personnel, including contracted personnel assigned to the  
school district, shall maintain a posture of complete neutrality with 
respect to all candidate(s), political issues, and/or political 
organizations/committees while engaged in duties for the Troy School 
District or in contact with students or students’ parents. 
 
Further, absent the express written consent of the superintendent or 
his/her designee, all school personnel shall be restricted from utilizing 
Troy School District resources, technology, including District e-mail, and 
student/parent contact information received while engaged in duties for 
the Troy School District for commercial purposes or for the purpose of 
supporting any candidate, candidates, slate of candidates, political 
issues, including any issues which cause substantial and material 
interference and/or disruption with normal school activities, or political 
organizations/committees.

K-1304 SERVICE ADVERTISING 
 
 
 Advertising of a service to employees or service to students may be allowed by 
obtaining approval of the superintendent or designee.  
 
 ADVERTISING ON SCHOOL DISTRICT PROPERTY AND IN SCHOOL 
DISTRICT ELECTRONIC MEDIA  
 
The school district property and electronic media, like school newspapers, 
yearbooks, and other District-sponsored publications and property, is a nonpublic 
forum subject to the District’s control and regulation.  The District will accept 
commercial advertisements, subject to the following rules and procedures. 
 
• Advertising is restricted to commercial purposes.  (The District reserves the 
right to give preference to local businesses and businesses that sell goods and 
services that students may be interested in purchasing.)  The District property 
and Web site are not forums for political, religious, or public advocacy 
discussion or expression. 
 
• The District reserves the right to deny advertisements that are ungrammatical, 
poorly written, inadequately researched, biased or prejudiced, vulgar or 
profane or otherwise unsuitable for student audiences. 
 
• Advertisements cannot be for goods or services which are illegal. 
 
• The sponsors, goods, services, advertisement or activities may not violate or 
condone (explicitly or implicitly) the violation of Board or School District’s 
policies, or be inconsistent with or in competition with the educational mission 
of the School District. 
 
• The advertisements cannot be libelous, offensive, contain explicit sexual 
content, or be obscene. 
 
• The District will not approve advertising in connection with the following 
products or uses: 
 
1.  Products used to injure or kill. 
2.  Alcohol-related products. 
3.  Tobacco-related products. 
4.  Illegal drug-related products.

5.  Prescription or over-the-counter medication. 
6.  Sexually suggestive products or language. 
7.  Gambling-related products. 
8.  Products that present an unacceptable risk of liability. 
9.  Products that are harmful to or inconsistent with the mission or image of 
the School District. 
10.  Racist, sexist, hateful, demeaning or degrading language or statements. 
11.  Profanity. 
12.  Sexual acts. 
13.  Statements impugning any school in the Troy School District or schools 
outside of the Troy School District. 
14.  Art or a design incorporating trademarks or copyrights owned by third 
parties, unless written permission of such use is satisfactory in form and 
substance to the school, is obtained from the trademark holder or 
copyright owner, and approved in advance. 
15.  Any use which suggests or implies the school district’s endorsement of 
other organizations, companies, products, services, political parties or 
views, or religious organizations or beliefs. 
16.  Any use that discriminates or implies discrimination against any persons 
or groups based on age, ancestry, belief, color, creed, disability, national 
origin, race, ethnicity, religion, sex, sexual orientation or veteran status, or 
in any other way that would be a violation of the School District’s anti-
discrimination policies or practices. 
 
All advertisements are to be reviewed by the superintendent or designee who is 
responsible for applying and enforcing this policy.  Any entity or person who 
disagrees with the superintendent or designee’s decision can petition the 
superintendent’s office for a review within 30 calendar days from the initial 
written decision of the designee.  The superintendent, or the superintendent’s 
designee, will review the petition and upon request meet with the petitioner.  The 
superintendent or designee will issue a decision within 30 calendar days after the 
petition and/or meeting which decision shall be final.
