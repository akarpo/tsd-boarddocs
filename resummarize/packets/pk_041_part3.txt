Based on building feedback of breakdowns and non-operable copiers, Merri Lynn Colligan, in 
the fall of 2012, began a process of identifying which copier models throughout the district 
were in the worst shape and which were the most costly to maintain and operate based on 
repeated breakdowns and/or service calls by buildings. Additionally, Konica has indicated to us 
that parts are no longer available to service these machines. The district has 22 copiers that 
have been identified as “End of Life” that are in dire need of being replaced. The district owns 
these machines.  
I sought quotes and existing bids by other governmental entities to replace these 22 machines. 
I located an existing bid with the Livingston Educational Service Agency (LESA) which was with 
Konica Minolta, our current vendor for the majority of our district copier machines. The LESA 
agreement is a five year pricing agreement which began in 2010 and is valid until 2/12/15.  
An analysis was done on lease-to-own vs. purchase of the models that are being considered. 
The result of this analysis was in support of purchasing the machines, since the total purchase 
price for 22 copiers is $25,000 less than the cost of leasing the machines on a 36 month lease-
to-own contract. The additional cost of a service agreement of .0039 cents per copy is the same 
for a 5 year purchase contract or a 36 month lease-to-own contract. The service contract 
includes all maintenance, repairs, and toner for these 22 machines for the 5 year contract 
period. 
Recommendation: 
Based on our analysis the Technology Department recommends the purchase of 22 Konica 
multi-function copiers (see attached schedule for models and locations) from Konica Minolta in 
the amount of $250,796. The award includes a five (5) year service agreement component 
based on the actual copies per machine, estimated to be $44,250/annually. The funding for this 
purchase and annual service agreement charges will be taken out of the General Fund budget.
