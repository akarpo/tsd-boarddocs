Secondary Science Materials Adoption: High School Science May 2019 Submitted by Kate Rainey and High School Science Teachers  Rationale This materials adoption is a part of the Troy School District regular seven-year adoption cycle.  Our current secondary science materials are out of date and are not aligned with the Michigan Science Standards approved by the State of Michigan School Board (2015). The Michigan Science Standards are a state-approved version of the Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS).    Current research indicates that students learn science by engaging in work as scientists. In a highly effective learning environment, students work collaboratively to develop evidence-based explanations of real-life phenomena that create a cohesive and compelling storyline. Classrooms are shifting away from places where students learn about science by memorizing facts to places where students figure out science by generating questions, designing investigations, analyzing evidence, and communicating their findings to others (See Figure 1. A New Vision for Science Education).   Process of Materials Selection Our process of materials adoption really started years ago when we began professional learning around the expectations of the new science standards. All high school teachers were engaged in reviewing texts and laboratory materials. The EQUIP rubric, published by Achieve and aligned to NGSS, was used to evaluate text materials. The review process took place over a full year and many teachers tested lessons and equipment in their classrooms. Priority was given to materials that are aligned to the NGSS and allow students the opportunity to investigate phenomena through compelling storylines.    Features of Recommended Texts and Equipment In March 2019, all teachers were asked to develop a list of features of the text series and equipment they were recommending as well as to provide a rationale for why other texts/materials were not chosen.  The features of the resources reviewed, piloted and recommended, are summarized in Table 1 (pages 4-19).    Textbook Recommendation Each course group came to consensus around text and material choices, and the recommended materials are summarized in Table 2 (pages 19 and 20) with projected costs.  Publisher quotes are included at the end of this document.  Copies of select texts from each series are available for review in the Teaching and Learning Workroom in the Services Building.
